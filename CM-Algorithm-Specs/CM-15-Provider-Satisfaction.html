Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-15 — Provider Satisfaction and Usability

US — User Satisfaction Provider Usability Also known as: Physician Satisfaction  ·  System Usability Scale (SUS)  ·  Ease of Use

Unit

Unit
System Usability Scale (SUS) score, 0–100 (68 = industry average)
↑ Higher is better

Provider-reported satisfaction with the ambient AI scribe tool, including perceived usability, ease of use, and overall acceptability — a subjective technology evaluation. Distinct from burnout (CM-01, an emotional/wellbeing outcome), from adoption behavior (CM-13, what providers actually do), and from adoption intention (CM-14, stated intent to continue). Recommendation likelihood and long-term-use items were reclassified into this measure during corpus derivation as closer to satisfaction than to intention.

Gold Standard
System Usability Scale (SUS) — validated 10-item instrument, 5-point Likert per item, scored 0–100 (industry average ≈ 68). Standard scoring: for odd-numbered items, subtract 1 from the response; for even-numbered items, subtract the response from 5; sum all 10 adjusted scores (range 0–40) and multiply by 2.5. The only formally named validated usability instrument in the corpus (Shah 2024).
Default / Practical
Ad hoc single- or multi-item Likert satisfaction scales, and UTAUT perceived-usefulness / perceived-ease-of-use constructs — the pattern actually used by 20 of the 21 papers in the corpus, since only one paper explicitly names the SUS.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • No meaningful pre-exposure baseline. Unlike burnout (CM-01), usability cannot be rated before any use of the tool — there is no true "Phase 0" for SUS. The design below compares an early-experience wave (weeks 4–8) to a mature-experience wave (months 4–9), not a pre/post-adoption change.
  • Volunteer/self-selection inflation. Satisfaction is almost universally positive across the corpus, but most studies recruited volunteers or interested practitioners. Prasad 2025 is the critical counter-signal: providers with <2 years of virtual-scribe use showed satisfaction strongly negatively associated with tenure. Administer to a mandatory or randomly-sampled cohort, not an opt-in one, wherever feasible.
  • Product satisfaction vs. occupational satisfaction mislabeling. Some corpus aliases ("Employee Satisfaction," "Job Satisfaction" in Boyter 2025 and Galloway 2024) actually measure satisfaction with the AI product, not occupational/job satisfaction in the general sense. Item wording must make clear the referent is the tool, not the job, to avoid this conflation.
  • Instrument inconsistency across the corpus. Only Shah 2024 explicitly names the standardized SUS; the remaining 20 papers use non-standardized ad hoc items, limiting cross-study comparability. Standardizing on SUS for any Suki-sponsored evaluation would materially improve benchmarking.

Algorithm

1 SUS Instrument Scoring — Dose-Response Cohort Comparison Early-experience / mature-experience administration

Produces a cohort-level SUS score for each administration wave, compared across Suki utilization tiers. The score is expected to increase (or stay stable at an already-favorable level) from early experience to mature use, with the largest increase in the heaviest-utilization cohort.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
System Usability Scale (SUS) score (0–100)
The canonical unit for this measure — defined in the Unit card above. Computed per respondent per wave using the standard SUS scoring formula, then averaged within each utilization-tier cohort.
Secondary
Suki utilization tier (heavy / moderate / light)
Per the PHTI 2025 framework: heavy (≥70% of eligible encounters with a completed Suki session), moderate (30–69%), light (<30%), assigned per provider by week 4 of adoption and held fixed for the comparison window.
⚠ Tier assignment is the only place Suki contributes primary data to this algorithm.
SUS Score=[ Σ (adjusted item score, 0–4, across 10 items) ] × 2.5
Item adjustment: odd items (1,3,5,7,9) -> adjusted = raw response (1-5) minus 1 even items (2,4,6,8,10) -> adjusted = 5 minus raw response (1-5) Administration: Wave 1 (early experience, weeks 4-8 post go-live) -- Wave 2 (mature use, months 4-9) Cohort: heavy / moderate / light Suki utilization tier, assigned at week 4 of adoption Reporting: cohort-mean SUS score per wave; benchmark against industry average of 68 (Brooke 1996) Sampling: prefer mandatory or randomly-sampled respondent pools over opt-in to limit volunteer-inflation bias
Pseudocode
Wave 1 (W1, weeks 4-8, all eligible providers): for item i in 1..10: adjusted[i] = response[i] - 1 if i is odd adjusted[i] = 5 - response[i] if i is even score[p, W1] = SUM(adjusted[1..10]) * 2.5 # range 0-100 Weeks 1-16: track Suki sessions per provider per week utilization_pct[p] = completed sessions / eligible encounters (rolling 4-week) tier[p] = HEAVY if utilization_pct[p] >= 0.70 MODERATE if 0.30 <= utilization_pct[p] < 0.70 LIGHT if utilization_pct[p] < 0.30 (assigned at week 4, held fixed) Wave 2 (W2, months 4-9, same respondents where possible): score[p, W2] = SUM(adjusted[1..10]) * 2.5 For each tier T in {HEAVY, MODERATE, LIGHT}: cohort_score[T, W1] = MEAN(score[p, W1] for p in tier T) cohort_score[T, W2] = MEAN(score[p, W2] for p in tier T) dose_response_delta[T] = cohort_score[T, W2] - cohort_score[T, W1] dashboard_value = cohort_score[T, W2] vs industry benchmark (68) and dose_response_delta[HEAVY] vs dose_response_delta[LIGHT]
Expected Trend — SUS Score / Cohort / Wave

Cohort-mean SUS score is expected to increase (or hold near an already-favorable level) from early experience (weeks 4–8) to mature use (months 4–9), with the largest increase in the heavy-utilization cohort. Interpret any absolute score against the industry benchmark of 68 (Brooke 1996), and recall that most corpus satisfaction estimates come from volunteer samples that plausibly overstate the true population mean.

Data Requirements

Data Element Description Source Notes
SUS survey responses (Wave 1) 10-item, 5-point scale, administered weeks 4–8 post go-live to all eligible providers. Survey Administered by customer or research team; Suki has no in-app distribution mechanism.
SUS survey responses (Wave 2) Same instrument, administered months 4–9 post go-live to the same respondent pool where possible. Survey Alternative: ad hoc Likert satisfaction items if full SUS is impractical — reduces cross-study comparability.
Sessions per provider per week Completed Suki session counts, used to compute rolling utilization percentage and assign heavy/moderate/light tier at week 4. Suki GET /session/{id}/status (aggregated). Defines the independent variable for the dose-response design.
Session status distribution completed vs. skipped vs. failed rate per provider — an early-friction proxy that may anticipate low usability ratings before survey results arrive. Suki GET /session/{id}/status.
Suki adoption date Date of each provider's first completed Suki session. Anchors the utilization-tier tracking window. Suki Wave 1 survey should follow this date by 4–8 weeks to allow a genuine first impression to form.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's role for CM-15 is limited to utilization-cohort scaffolding and early-friction screening. Session counts and timing define the heavy/moderate/light dose-response cohorts, and skip/fail rate trends offer a rough early-warning proxy for usability friction. Suki has no native satisfaction- or usability-rating data — this is a provider-reported subjective evaluation with no API analog.

The SUS (or an ad hoc satisfaction scale) must be administered and captured by the customer or research team, on the schedule above. Because a true pre-exposure baseline is not meaningful for a usability rating, CM-15 should be reported as an early-vs-mature experience comparison, not a pre/post-adoption change.
