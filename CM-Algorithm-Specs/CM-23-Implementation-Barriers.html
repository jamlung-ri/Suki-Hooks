Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-23 — Implementation Barriers and Workflow Fit

SerQ — Service Quality Implementation & Workflow Fit Also known as: Implementation Barriers  ·  Learning Curve  ·  Workflow Adaptation

Unit

Unit
FIM/AIM/IAM composite implementation-outcome score (1–5) + qualitative barrier theme frequency
↑ Higher fit / fewer barriers is better

Factors that impede or facilitate ambient AI scribe adoption and sustained use, including technical barriers, workflow integration challenges, specialty fit, training quality, and organizational support. Mapped to the Service Quality (SerQ) dimension as a property of the deployment/implementation experience, distinct from the tool's own usability (CM-15) or the provider's stated intention to keep using it (CM-14). This measure uniquely combines a validated quantitative implementation-outcome scale with a qualitative barrier-coding component — neither is sufficient alone.

Gold Standard
Feasibility of Intervention Measure (FIM) / Acceptability of Intervention Measure (AIM) / Intervention Appropriateness Measure (IAM) — each a 4-item, 1–5 Likert scale (Weiner et al. 2017), reported in the corpus via Suhail 2026.
Default / Practical
Structured qualitative-mixed-methods assessment against an implementation-science framework (CFIR, RE-AIM, or SEIPS 3.0) plus non-adopter category analysis — the pattern used by most of the 12 corpus papers, since only one names a formally scored instrument.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • FIM/AIM/IAM sourcing is itself flagged as unverified in the underlying literature review. The corpus notes state: "unclear where Suhail 2026 is sourcing FIM/AIM/IUS from, check that these scales appear in the paper and are correctly attributed." Do not treat this instrument as confirmed corpus-validated until independently verified against the primary source.
  • "IUS" is an undefined acronym in the source material — possibly a typo for IAM, or a distinct scale. Unresolved; do not field an "IUS" instrument without first clarifying what it refers to.
  • Heterogeneous frameworks are not interchangeable. RE-AIM, CFIR, SEIPS 3.0, and QUEST are structurally different mixed-methods frameworks, not alternate scoring systems for the same number. Pick one framework per deployment and hold it constant across administration waves.
  • Non-adopter survivorship bias. Providers who fully disengage from Suki are also the least likely to respond to any follow-up survey, biasing barrier data toward moderate/partial users rather than true non-adopters — the group implementation barriers matter most for.
  • No standardized qualitative barrier taxonomy exists in the corpus. AAFP 2021 uses 4 non-adopter types, PHTI 2025 uses 3 adoption cohorts, and other papers use free-form thematic analysis. Any dashboard-level "top barriers" frequency count requires establishing a project-specific coding taxonomy first.

Algorithm

1 FIM/AIM/IAM Instrument Scoring — Dose-Response Cohort Comparison Early-adoption / mature-use administration

Produces a cohort-level implementation-outcome composite score for each administration wave, compared across Suki utilization tiers, including a non-adopter cohort unique to this measure. As with usability (CM-15), a tool's implementation fit cannot be rated before any exposure, so the first wave is early adoption, not a true pre-deployment baseline. The composite is expected to increase from early adoption to mature use, most in the heaviest-utilization cohort.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
FIM/AIM/IAM composite score (1–5)
The canonical unit for this measure — defined in the Unit card above. Computed as the mean of the 12 items (4 Feasibility + 4 Acceptability + 4 Appropriateness) per respondent per wave; each subscale is also reported individually, then averaged within each cohort.
⚠ Instrument sourcing is unverified — see Ambiguities above.
Secondary
Suki utilization tier (heavy / moderate / light / non-adopter)
Per the PHTI 2025 framework: heavy (≥70% of eligible encounters with a completed Suki session), moderate (30–69%), light (<30%), assigned per provider by week 4 of adoption. A fourth non-adopter category (never completed a session, or discontinued entirely) is retained specifically for this measure, unlike CM-01/CM-14/CM-15/CM-16 where non-adopters are typically excluded.
⚠ Non-adopters are the hardest cohort to survey — see the survivorship-bias ambiguity above.
Composite Score=∑ (item response, 1–5) across 12 FIM/AIM/IAM items12
Administration: Wave 1 (early adoption, weeks 4-8 post go-live) -- Wave 2 (mature use, months 4-9) Cohort: heavy / moderate / light / non-adopter Suki utilization tier, assigned at week 4 Reporting: cohort-mean composite and per-subscale (FIM, AIM, IAM) score per wave; report dose-response delta (heavy minus light) and non-adopter barrier profile separately Sampling: actively pursue non-adopter response (e.g., via non-Suki-channel outreach) to limit survivorship bias
Pseudocode
Wave 1 (W1, weeks 4-8, all eligible providers incl. non-adopters): score[p, W1] = MEAN(FIM_items + AIM_items + IAM_items, 1-5) for provider p # 12 items Weeks 1-16: track Suki sessions per provider per week utilization_pct[p] = completed sessions / eligible encounters (rolling 4-week) tier[p] = HEAVY if utilization_pct[p] >= 0.70 MODERATE if 0.30 <= utilization_pct[p] < 0.70 LIGHT if 0 < utilization_pct[p] < 0.30 NON_ADOPTER if utilization_pct[p] == 0 (never completed a session, or discontinued) (assigned at week 4, held fixed) Wave 2 (W2, months 4-9, same respondents where possible, esp. non-adopters): score[p, W2] = MEAN(FIM_items + AIM_items + IAM_items, 1-5) for provider p For each tier T in {HEAVY, MODERATE, LIGHT, NON_ADOPTER}: cohort_score[T, W1] = MEAN(score[p, W1] for p in tier T) cohort_score[T, W2] = MEAN(score[p, W2] for p in tier T) dose_response_delta[T] = cohort_score[T, W2] - cohort_score[T, W1] dashboard_value = dose_response_delta[HEAVY] vs dose_response_delta[LIGHT] reported alongside cohort_score[NON_ADOPTER] as the barrier-severity floor
2 Qualitative Barrier Coding — Theme Frequency by Cohort Companion to Algorithm 1, not merged into the composite score

Codes semi-structured interview or open-response survey data against a bespoke barrier taxonomy (technical, workflow-integration, specialty-fit, training, organizational support), then reports theme frequency stratified by adoption cohort — especially non-adopters vs. heavy users — to surface which barriers most differentiate successful from unsuccessful implementation.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
Barrier theme code (technical / workflow / specialty-fit / training / organizational)
Assigned per respondent from interview transcripts or open-response survey items, following the taxonomy pattern observed across the corpus (e.g., AAFP 2021's 4 non-adopter types, Bundy 2024's encounter-suitability theme).
⚠ No single shared taxonomy exists across the corpus — define and hold constant a project-specific rubric before coding.
Secondary
Adoption cohort (from Algorithm 1)
Used to stratify theme frequency — the corpus's universal finding is heterogeneous adoption with distinct heavy, moderate, and non-/low-user cohorts, and clinicians who benefit most share high baseline documentation burden, complex narrative notes, and high patient face-time; non-adopters cluster around low baseline burden, already-optimized workflow, or technical incompatibility.
Theme Frequency [T]=Count of respondents in cohort T citing themeTotal respondents coded in cohort T
Grain: one frequency table per theme category, cross-tabulated by cohort (heavy / moderate / light / non-adopter) Reporting: rank themes within the non-adopter cohort separately -- this is the cohort implementation-improvement effort should target first Not a numeric score to average with Algorithm 1's composite -- report side by side
Pseudocode
taxonomy = { TECHNICAL, WORKFLOW_INTEGRATION, SPECIALTY_FIT, TRAINING, ORG_SUPPORT } for each respondent p with interview/open-response data: themes[p] = CODE_AGAINST_TAXONOMY(transcript_or_response[p], taxonomy) # 1+ themes per respondent for each cohort T in {HEAVY, MODERATE, LIGHT, NON_ADOPTER}: for each theme K in taxonomy: theme_frequency[T, K] = COUNT(p in T where K in themes[p]) / COUNT(p in T) dashboard_value = theme_frequency[NON_ADOPTER, *] ranked descending (report cross-cohort comparison to identify which themes most distinguish non-adopters from heavy users -- the actionable barrier signal)
Expected Trend — FIM/AIM/IAM Composite Score / Cohort / Wave

Cohort-mean composite implementation-fit score is expected to increase from early adoption to mature use as training and workflow habituation progress, most in the heavy-utilization cohort. The non-adopter cohort is expected to remain flat and low across both waves — per the corpus's universal finding, non-adopters cluster around low baseline documentation burden, an already-optimized workflow, or technical incompatibility, none of which resolve simply through more exposure.

Data Requirements

Data Element Description Source Notes
FIM/AIM/IAM survey responses (Wave 1) 12-item composite, administered weeks 4–8 post go-live to all eligible providers, including those who have not completed a session. Survey Instrument sourcing unverified — confirm against Weiner et al. 2017 before fielding.

Onboarding / training completion records Whether and when a provider completed initial Suki onboarding — a potential covariate for implementation-fit scoring. Suki Availability depends on whether Suki's customer success/onboarding system tracks this; confirm before relying on it.
Suki adoption date (or non-adoption confirmation) Date of first completed Suki session, or confirmation that a provider never completed one. Anchors the tier and cohort tracking window. Suki Needed to distinguish true non-adopters from providers simply not yet reached.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's role for CM-23 is limited to utilization-cohort scaffolding and, if tracked, onboarding/training completion records. Session counts and timing define the heavy/moderate/light/non-adopter cohorts, and session skip/fail-rate trends offer a rough early-friction proxy. Suki has no native implementation-outcome or barrier-coding data — this measure is entirely provider-reported (survey and interview), with no API analog for feasibility, acceptability, appropriateness, or workflow-fit perception.

Reaching the non-adopter cohort is the hardest part of this measure operationally: if Suki has no in-app or in-product channel to reach providers who never engaged, non-adopter outreach must run entirely through the customer's own communication channels, independent of Suki. The FIM/AIM/IAM instrument and qualitative barrier interviews must be administered and captured by the customer or research team, on the schedule above.
