Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-10 — Note Quality Overall

IQ — Information Quality Composite / Rollup Measure Also known as: PDQI-9 Score  ·  PDSQI-9  ·  SAIL Score  ·  Overall PDQI Score  ·  Global Rating  ·  Documentation Quality

Unit

Unit
Composite PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 quality score (9 domains, 1–5 each; range 9–45)
↑ Higher is better

An aggregate quality rating of the AI-generated note across all nine PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 domains: Accuracy, Thoroughness, Usefulness, Organization, Comprehensibility, Succinctness, Synthesis, Consistency, and an overall impression item. It is explicitly a rollup: two of its nine domains are independently tracked as their own canonical measures elsewhere in this corpus — Accuracy is CM-09 (Note Inaccuracy Rate) and Thoroughness is CM-08 (Note Completeness). The remaining six domains (Usefulness, Organization, Comprehensibility, Succinctness, Synthesis, Consistency) are captured only through this composite.

Gold Standard
PDQI-9 human-rater composite — the original validated 9-item instrument, 5-point Likert per item (max 45), designed for human-authored notes and widely used in simulation studies as the reference standard.
Default / Practical
PDSQI-9 LLM-as-judge composite — the AI-note-adapted version. Afshar 2025b validated an LLM-as-judge implementation against physician ratings (3.97–4.99/5.0 in that RCT), making fully automated composite scoring feasible at scale once agreement is established.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Raw scores are not self-interpreting. Kernberg 2023 reports approximate scale anchors of 26.2 ≈ "terrible or bad" and 36.6 ≈ "good or excellent" (their study mean: 29.7, closer to terrible) — without publishing the anchor points alongside a raw composite number, the score cannot be read as pass/fail or good/bad on its own.
  • Composite masks which domain moved. Because Accuracy (CM-09) and Thoroughness (CM-08) are two of the nine inputs, a stable or improving composite score can hide a decline in one domain offset by a gain in another. Always report the composite alongside its component domain scores, not as a replacement for them.
  • SAIL score is a different instrument. SAIL is a separate standards-based scoring approach, not numerically equivalent to a PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 sum or mean. Do not average or convert between instruments; report on separate tracks if both are collected.
  • Per-section and per-note-type scoring variants exist. Some studies apply PDQI by note section (e.g., SOAP Subjective / Plan) or by note type (progress note vs. discharge summary) rather than to the whole note. These variants are not interchangeable with a whole-note composite.

Algorithm

1 Human Rating / LLM-as-Judge Scoring — PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 Composite Pilot Rapid pilot → scale on agreement

A staged pilot that pulls a sample of session transcript/note pairs, has 2–3 physician raters score all nine PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 domains per note, runs an LLM-as-judge prompt on the same rubric over the same pairs, and — if human/LLM agreement is sufficient (r ≥ 0.7) — scales the LLM-as-judge composite scoring to the full session corpus. This is the same rating pipeline used for CM-08 and CM-09; CM-10 reports the full nine-domain composite rather than a single domain.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 composite score (sum of 9 domains, range 9–45; reported as mean 1–5)
Scored per note by physician raters during the pilot; scored per note by an LLM judge using the identical rubric for both the pilot calibration set and (if agreement is sufficient) the full corpus. Reported as a cohort mean composite value.
Secondary
Per-domain subscale scores (Accuracy, Thoroughness, Usefulness, Organization, Comprehensibility, Succinctness, Synthesis, Consistency)
The nine individual 1–5 domain scores that sum to the composite. Accuracy is reported separately as CM-09; Thoroughness is reported separately as CM-08.
⚠ Always compute and retain per-domain scores, not only the composite — without them, a change in the composite cannot be attributed to a specific quality dimension.
Secondary
SAIL score (alternate composite instrument)
A separate standards-based AI documentation quality score, collected if the pilot design includes it alongside PDQI-9/PDSQI-9.
⚠ Not numerically equivalent to the PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 composite — report on a separate track, do not blend into a single "quality score."
Secondary
Human–LLM agreement (r)
Pearson/Spearman correlation between physician-rater composite scores and LLM-as-judge composite scores on the same calibration sample. The gating value for whether automated scoring can be scaled to the full corpus.
⚠ Pipeline gap: as with CM-08 and CM-09, every score in this algorithm is computed against the transcript, not the clinician's final accepted note, until the EHR-side final note and edit history are available.
Composite Quality Score=∑ (domain score, across 9 PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 domains)9
Pilot design (shared with CM-08 / CM-09 / Afshar 2025b pattern): 1. Sample 50-100 completed session transcript/note pairs via source_transcripts[] 2. 2-3 physician raters score all 9 PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 domains (1-5 each) per note 3. LLM-as-judge scores the same pairs using the identical rubric 4. Compute human-LLM agreement r on the calibration sample, overall and per domain 5. If r >= 0.7: scale LLM-as-judge composite scoring to the full session corpus If r < 0.7: refine rubric/prompt and re-pilot before scaling Report the composite mean AND all 9 per-domain means -- never the composite alone Reporting cadence: pilot wave (one-time calibration) -> ongoing (if scaled), by cohort / utilization tier
Pseudocode
sample = SELECT 50..100 completed sessions WHERE source_transcripts[] is non-empty DOMAINS = ["Accuracy", "Thoroughness", "Usefulness", "Organization", "Comprehensibility", "Succinctness", "Synthesis", "Consistency", "Overall"] for session in sample: pairs[session] = (session.transcript, session.note_content, session.source_transcripts) # Human + LLM rating, all 9 domains for session in sample: human_domain_scores[session] = { d: MEAN(rater.score(d, pairs[session]) for rater in raters) for d in DOMAINS } llm_domain_scores[session] = { d: LLM_JUDGE(d + "_rubric", pairs[session]) for d in DOMAINS } human_composite[session] = MEAN(human_domain_scores[session].values()) llm_composite[session] = MEAN(llm_domain_scores[session].values()) agreement_r = CORRELATION(human_composite, llm_composite) # across sample agreement_r_by_domain = { d: CORRELATION([human_domain_scores[s][d] for s in sample], [llm_domain_scores[s][d] for s in sample]) for d in DOMAINS } if agreement_r >= 0.7: # scale: apply LLM_JUDGE to all_completed_sessions composite_score = MEAN(llm_composite[s] for s in all_completed_sessions) domain_scores = { d: MEAN(llm_domain_scores[s][d] for s in all_completed_sessions) for d in DOMAINS } else: composite_score = MEAN(human_composite[s] for s in sample) # pilot-only estimate domain_scores = { d: MEAN(human_domain_scores[s][d] for s in sample) for d in DOMAINS } dashboard_value = { "composite_score": composite_score, "domain_scores": domain_scores, "agreement_r": agreement_r }
Expected Trend — Composite PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 Score / Cohort / Month

The composite score is expected to rise after Suki adoption if its component domains improve — most directly Thoroughness (CM-08) and Accuracy (CM-09). Because the composite has no universal pass/fail cut point, read the trend against instrument-specific anchors reported in the literature (Kernberg 2023: ~26 ≈ poor, ~37 ≈ good on a comparable 9-item scale), not as an absolute target.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's source_transcripts[], transcript, and note content endpoints supply the transcript/note pairs that the composite rating pipeline is built on — the same pairs used for the CM-08 and CM-09 pilots.

Suki has no native survey or human-rating collection mechanism, and does not compute a PDQI-9/PDSQI-9/SAIL composite natively; the 9-domain scoring and composite calculation happen entirely at the rating-pipeline level (human raters plus LLM-as-judge), following the same Afshar 2025b calibration pattern used for CM-08/CM-09. As with those measures, the Missing Feedback Loop means every composite score in this card is computed relative to the transcript, not the clinician's final signed note, until an EHR integration or data-sharing provision closes that gap.

⚠ Verified against Epic's real EHI schema (not the vendor-metrics-catalog guesses this card previously relied on): HNO_INFO and NOTES_HISTORY_LOG exist and give note metadata and edit-event timestamps, but no verified Epic table in this schema stores full-text note version snapshots or a Suki-draft-vs-final diff — closing the Missing Feedback Loop for CM-10 (as for CM-08/CM-09) would require a table type not found here, not just access to one that already exists.
