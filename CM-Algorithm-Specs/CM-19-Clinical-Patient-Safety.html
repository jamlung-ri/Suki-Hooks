Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-19 — Clinical Patient Safety

OI — Organizational Impact Monitor — 6/12 (High Actionability) Scope Narrowing Applies Also known as: AHRQ Harm Score  ·  Patient Safety Events  ·  Likelihood of Psychiatric Intervention (reclassification candidate)

Unit

Unit
AHRQ Harm Score / adverse-event rate (gold standard) — documentation-completeness ↔ clinical-action odds ratio (near-term proxy)
↓ Lower harm = better

Patient harm outcomes associated with ambient AI documentation — adverse events, AHRQ harm scores, and gaps between what gets documented and what clinical action actually follows. The canonical measures corpus calls this "the most concerning and least studied outcome dimension" — only 9 papers touch it, most using simulated or survey data rather than real patient outcomes. H1 (ambient documentation) itself is rated ○ (none) for CM-19 — ambient note generation alone does not plausibly move a harm outcome. H8 (orders staging) and H11 (chart Q&A) are the hooks that introduce safety-relevant pathways.

Gold Standard
AHRQ Harm Score / formal adverse-event and incident reports — typically live in administrative/risk-management systems separate from the EHR proper, and are likely the hardest data of any canonical measure to access.
Default / Practical
Castro 2025 replication design: an adjusted odds ratio (aOR) linking a Suki-native documentation-completeness signal to EHR order/intervention data for the same encounters. Not itself a CM-19 finding until paired with a downstream clinical-action or harm signal — see Scope Narrowing below.
⚠ Scope Narrowing (Critical — Read Before Reporting Any Error-Rate Data)
  • True patient safety requires measured patient harm outcomes. Errors in documentation that do not demonstrate a downstream patient outcome are technical quality issues (→ CM-09 Note Inaccuracy Rate), not safety. Severity of error is not patient safety without evidence of patient harm.
  • Several commonly-cited "safety" items are likely misclassified absent outcome evidence: "Clinically Significant Errors in AI Notes" (Biro 2025, Anderson 2025) → CM-09; "Negation Errors" (Bundy 2024) → CM-09; RDoC/PHQ-9 documentation level (Castro 2025) → CM-08; "Likelihood of Psychiatric Intervention, aOR" (Castro 2025) → quality-of-care/clinical-decision-rate, adjacent to but not itself safety.
  • Suki's own evidence-linking and note-accuracy work (CM-09's H15/source_transcripts[]) will generate data that looks safety-relevant but should be reported under CM-09/CM-08 unless paired with an observed clinical action or harm signal.
  • H8 and H11 are the relevant future hooks for CM-19, not H1. Any CM-19 evaluation plan should be scoped against H8/H11 deployment timelines, not current production capability.
  • Pearlman/Castro-style aOR findings cut both directions. A significant aOR (e.g., Castro 2025's 0.83, p=.005 — more documentation, less intervention) is a potential unintended consequence, not automatically "bad" without further investigation — but it is the kind of signal CM-19 needs.

Algorithm

1 Linkage / Association Design — Documentation Completeness vs. Clinical Action (Castro 2025 Replication) Becomes a CM-19 finding only with EHR linkage

Replicates the only landmark CM-19 design pattern in the literature: Castro 2025 found that ambient scribe use was associated with more psychiatric symptom documentation but significantly less psychiatric intervention (aOR 0.83, p=.005, preprint only). The design links a documented signal (documentation completeness — something Suki's structured-data output plausibly increases) to a clinical action signal (intervention rate — something only the EHR can provide), then tests the association via logistic regression.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary (available today)
Documentation-completeness signal
Per encounter: presence of a relevant symptom/diagnosis as a structured code in Suki's PBC/ICD-10 output (the Suki-side analog of Castro 2025's RDoC level / PHQ-9 documentation). Available today via GET /session/{id}/structured-data — no new instrumentation required.
⚠ On its own, this signal is a CM-08 (Note Completeness) finding, not CM-19 — see Scope Narrowing.
Secondary (the missing link)
EHR order/intervention rate for the same encounters
E.g., psychiatric referral, medication order, follow-up scheduling — the clinical-action half of the design. Requires encounter-level linkage between Suki session_id and EHR order data.
⚠ Pipeline gap (the single biggest structural gap for CM-19): without encounter-level linkage to EHR orders/interventions, no Suki-native documentation signal can be tested against a clinical-action outcome — which is the only thing that makes a finding "CM-19" rather than "CM-08/CM-09." H8's order-staging audit trail (staged vs. manual, accepted/edited/rejected) is the future hook most likely to close this, once it ships.
Secondary
Adjusted odds ratio (aOR) of intervention given documentation completeness
The actual CM-19-candidate output — logistic regression of intervention occurrence on documentation-completeness level, adjusted for covariates (visit type, provider specialty, patient demographics). Only valid once the linkage above exists.
aOR=odds(intervention | high documentation completeness)odds(intervention | low documentation completeness) (adjusted for covariates)
Step 1 (available today): compute documentation_completeness[encounter] from Suki structured-data output Step 2 (missing link): join encounter to EHR order/intervention record -> intervention[encounter] (0/1) Step 3: logistic regression intervention ~ documentation_completeness + covariates -> aOR + 95% CI Interpretation guardrail: aOR significantly != 1 is a CM-19-relevant signal (association between documentation and clinical action) — but per Scope Narrowing, frame as hypothesis-generating replication of Castro 2025, not as a harm measurement, unless an actual harm/incident outcome is also available for the same encounters
Pseudocode
For each completed session s: structured = GET /session/{s.id}/structured-data documentation_completeness[s] = HAS_RELEVANT_STRUCTURED_CODE(structured) # e.g., psych symptom/diagnosis code # [MISSING LINK — requires EHR integration] for each session s: intervention[s] = EHR_ORDER_OCCURRED(s.encounter_id, window = encounter_date..+N days) # e.g., psychiatric referral, medication order, follow-up scheduling covariates[s] = { visit_type, provider_specialty, patient_age_band, ... } # from EHR model = LOGISTIC_REGRESSION( outcome = intervention, predictor = documentation_completeness, covariates = covariates ) dashboard_value = { "aOR": model.odds_ratio("documentation_completeness"), "ci_95": model.confidence_interval("documentation_completeness"), "n_encounters": COUNT(sessions with both documentation_completeness and intervention available) } # If aOR significantly < 1 (replicating Castro 2025): flag for H8/H11-informed follow-up, # report as CM-19-relevant hypothesis, not as a harm measurement
2 Retrospective Annotation — CM-09 Pipeline (Open Question 5) Low-cost, prepares for future CM-19 identification

A lightweight addition to the CM-09 (Note Inaccuracy) PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 rating pipeline: when a human rater or LLM-as-judge identifies an error in an AI-generated note, also capture whether a downstream clinical action was observed as a result of that error. This does not require new EHR access beyond what CM-09 already needs for its rating sample, and creates a retrospective dataset that can be mined for CM-19 candidates once EHR order/intervention linkage (Algorithm 1) becomes available.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary (annotation field)
Downstream clinical action observed: yes / no / unknown
Added as a field on the CM-09 PDQI-9/PDSQI-9 rating instrument, completed by the same human rater / LLM-as-judge already reviewing the note for accuracy. "Unknown" is an acceptable and expected majority answer given the Missing Feedback Loop (CM-09) — raters typically only see the transcript and generated note, not the final chart.
Secondary
source_transcripts[] (H11 audit trail, if available)
Where H11 (chart Q&A) is in use, the interaction log provides a more direct trace of whether a documented item was acted upon — useful context for the rater's annotation.
Why this matters now: this annotation is nearly free to add to an already-planned CM-09 pilot, and turns "unknown" cases into a labeled backlog. If/when Algorithm 1's EHR linkage ships, this backlog can be re-queried for cases marked "yes" to bootstrap a CM-19 cohort without waiting for a new prospective study.
Expected Pattern — Documentation Completeness vs. Intervention Rate

Hypothesis to test (Castro 2025 replication), not an assumed result. Castro 2025 found higher documentation completeness associated with lower psychiatric intervention rates (aOR 0.83, p=.005) — a pattern worth detecting either direction.

Suki Data Scope

Nothing in Suki's current data measures CM-19 directly. The structured diagnoses/PBC output that looks most safety-relevant (/structured-data) is a CM-08 documentation-completeness signal until it can be linked to EHR order/intervention data for the same encounters — that linkage does not exist today and is the single largest structural gap blocking CM-19 measurement.

H1 (ambient documentation) is rated ○ (none) for CM-19 — ambient note generation alone does not plausibly move a harm outcome. The hooks that matter for CM-19 are H8 (order staging), the "highest-stakes new safety variable" once it ships, and H11 (chart Q&A), which "could improve or worsen safety depending on answer accuracy." Neither is in production today.

Recommended near-term path: (1) pursue an EHR order/intervention linkage pilot to enable the Castro 2025 replication design in Algorithm 1 — this is the highest-value single ask for this measure; (2) add the low-cost retrospective annotation from Algorithm 2 to the CM-09 pilot now, so a labeled backlog exists by the time linkage is available; (3) when reporting any error/inaccuracy findings from CM-09's evidence-linking work, hold the line on Scope Narrowing — do not report them as CM-19 patient-safety findings without an observed downstream clinical action.
