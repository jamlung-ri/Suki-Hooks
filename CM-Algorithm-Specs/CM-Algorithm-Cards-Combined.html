CM-04 — Documentation Time

NB — Net Benefits Documentation Efficiency Also known as: Time in Notes  ·  Note Completion Time  ·  Documentation Duration

Unit

Unit
Documentation minutes per encounter
↓ Lower is better

A documentation minute is any minute of activity attributable to note creation or editing for a given encounter — counted from encounter open through final note signature, including post-signature addenda. Measured via EHR audit-log telemetry (preferred) or Suki session duration (fallback). Each minute is attributed to the encounter in which the documentation occurred.

Gold Standard
EHR audit-log telemetry (e.g., Epic Signal / UAL) capturing time-stamped, per-user documentation activity bounded to note creation and editing. Preferred — objective and captures the full note-to-sign lifecycle inside the EHR.
Default / Practical
Suki session duration: start-to-end timestamp per dictation or ambient capture session. Use when EHR audit-log data are unavailable. Captures in-app documentation time only; misses editing and signing that occurs inside the EHR after the Suki session ends.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Per-encounter vs. per-note vs. per-day. Documentation time is reported per encounter, per note, or per provider per day across the literature. These units are not interchangeable. Choose one and apply it consistently across the pre- and post-adoption windows.
  • Self-report vs. objective telemetry. Self-reported documentation time overestimates improvement by 3–6× relative to EHR audit-log data (Ma 2025; PHTI 2025). Do not mix self-report and objective sources in the same analysis.
  • What counts as documentation time. Some EHR telemetry fields include inbox, chart review, or order entry in their “documentation time” metric. Confirm whether your EHR field is scoped to note creation and editing only, or to broader EHR activity. The latter overlaps with CM-07 (Total EHR Time).

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki session timestamps are the strongest Suki-native signal for this measure — session start and end times are recorded for every dictation and ambient capture session and are available directly from the Suki API. For organizations without EHR audit-log access, Suki session duration is a usable fallback proxy for in-app documentation time.

However, Suki session time does not capture the full documentation lifecycle. Note editing, formatting, and signing that occur inside the EHR after a Suki session closes are invisible to Suki. For the most accurate measure of total documentation time per encounter, use EHR audit-log telemetry; reserve Suki session duration as a fallback only.

CM-05 — After-Hours Documentation

NB — Net Benefits Documentation Efficiency Also known as: Pajama Time  ·  Work Outside Work  ·  After-Hours EHR Time

Unit

Unit
After-hours documentation minutes
↓ Lower is better

A documentation minute is after-hours if it falls outside the provider's working hours on that calendar day. Minutes are counted from encounter open through final note signature, including any post-signature addenda and edits.

Gold Standard
Per-provider or per-specialty working hours, as configured by the care setting. Preferred — avoids systematic misclassification across specialties with non-standard hours.
Default / Practical
Before 09:00 or after 17:00 on weekdays; any time on Saturday or Sunday. Use when per-provider scheduling data are unavailable.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Working hours vary by specialty. Surgeons may work 09:00–21:00; a 9–5 default over-counts after-hours time for them and cannot be compared across specialties without adjustment.
  • When does documentation end? We recommend counting through final signature and all addenda; stopping at initial note closure understates after-hours activity and is not directly comparable across implementations.
  • Pre-encounter prep vs. after-hours. Early chart review (e.g., 06:00 before rounds) may be classified as after-hours under a fixed window even within a provider's normal practice. Provider-level scheduling resolves this; the 9–5 default does not.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's contribution to this measure is the adoption boundary, not the documentation signal. Suki session data indicates when a dictation or ambient capture session was initiated, but it does not measure total EHR documentation time. Work within the EHR before or after a Suki session is invisible to Suki; note signing happens in the EHR, not in Suki.

The authoritative data source is the EHR audit log. Using Suki session timing as a proxy for after-hours documentation time would systematically undercount documentation minutes and miss chart review, note editing, and order entry that occurs outside any Suki session.

CM-07 — Total EHR Time

NB — Net Benefits EHR Efficiency Also known as: Total EHR Time  ·  Login Time  ·  Active EHR Time  ·  EHR Burden

Unit

Unit
Total EHR minutes per encounter
↓ Lower is better

A total EHR minute is any minute of logged activity in the EHR system by a given provider, across all task types — note creation, inbox management, order entry, results review, and any other EHR interaction. Minutes are summed per provider per encounter-equivalent period and normalized to completed encounters.

Gold Standard
Full EHR audit-log telemetry capturing all activity types across all EHR modules (documentation, inbox, orders, results, admin). Preferred — gives the broadest and most complete view of EHR burden.
Default / Practical
Documentation time + inbox time only, when full audit-log is unavailable. These two components are the most reliably extracted from most EHR telemetry systems and account for the largest share of total EHR time.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Scope of "EHR time." Different EHR telemetry fields vary in which activities they include. Before using any field, confirm whether it captures documentation only, documentation + inbox, or all EHR activity. Mixing narrow and broad definitions across pre/post windows invalidates the comparison. This measure is explicitly broader than CM-04 (Documentation Time).
  • Per-encounter vs. per-workday normalization. Some studies report total EHR time per day (Ma 2025: −19.95 min/day); others normalize to encounters. Per-encounter normalization is preferred when encounter volume itself may change after Suki adoption (see CM-22). Per-day is acceptable when encounter volume is stable.
  • Inbox and orders may dilute the Suki signal. Suki directly impacts documentation time; its effect on inbox and order entry is indirect and smaller. A large inbox component in the denominator will attenuate the observed effect size relative to a documentation-only measure. Report the component breakdown when possible.

Algorithm

1 Dashboard Metric — Cohort Average For the Suki admin console

Produces the average total EHR minutes per encounter per provider in the cohort for a given month. This is the broadest efficiency indicator in the CM suite: it captures all EHR time, not just documentation. Ma 2025 found a larger total-EHR-time reduction (−19.95 min/day) than documentation-time-alone reduction (−6.89 min/day) in the same cohort.

Suki provides a partial proxy for the documentation component of total EHR time, but cannot observe inbox activity, order entry, or results review. Session duration from the Suki API reflects in-app documentation time only. The majority of total EHR time — inbox management, chart review, and orders — occurs entirely within the EHR and is invisible to Suki.

Suki's primary contributions to this measure are the adoption date (pre/post boundary) and the active usage flag (denominator definition). For the outcome signal itself, the EHR full audit-log is the authoritative source. Organizations that report only Suki session duration for this measure will systematically understate total EHR time and the true magnitude of any change.

CM-20 — Financial Productivity

NB — Net Benefits Financial Productivity Operationalizations: RVUs per Encounter  ·  Billing Amount  ·  E/M Level Improvement

Measure Overview

Construct
Financial output per provider — three operationalizations
↑ Higher is better (all three)

Financial productivity captures whether Suki adoption translates into measurable revenue impact. The underlying mechanism is documentation completeness: faster, more thorough notes may enable providers to capture the full complexity of an encounter, support higher E/M billing levels, and reduce coding omissions that leave revenue on the table.

All three operationalizations measure financial output and are expected to increase after Suki adoption. They are complementary, not interchangeable — RVU output captures work intensity, billing revenue captures collected dollars, and E/M level distribution captures coding behavior. Use all three together when possible.

Three Operationalizations — See Algorithms Below
1wRVUs per provider per week — work intensity per encounter
2Paid revenue per provider per month ($) — collected dollars after adjustments
3% encounters billed at Level 4–5 — high-complexity coding capture rate
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve (All Three)
  • Case mix must be stable. Suki adoption does not change which patients a provider sees. A shift in patient complexity — independently of Suki — will change RVU output, revenue, and E/M levels. Use within-provider pre/post comparisons, and flag any known panel changes during the analysis window.
  • Use adjusted revenue, not gross charges. Gross charges vary by contract and payer mix. Adjusted net revenue (collected) is the meaningful signal. Pair with wRVU output to show the coding mechanism behind any revenue change.
  • Rising E/M levels must be read alongside denial rate (CM-21c). Increased billing at Level 4–5 should not be accompanied by increased denials. A denial rate that rises in step with coding levels is a signal of overcoding, not legitimate complexity capture. Read CM-20 and CM-21c together.
  • Report within specialty. wRVU weights, revenue, and E/M distributions vary substantially by specialty. Mixing specialties in one cohort without normalization produces uninterpretable averages.

Algorithms

1 wRVUs per Provider per Week Work intensity measure

Work relative value units (wRVUs) are the CMS-assigned measure of physician work per procedure. Summing wRVUs per provider per week provides a payer-neutral measure of productive output that controls for payer mix and contract variation. Holmgren 2026 found +0.04 wRVU/encounter (+$3,044/year) at UCSF; Boyter/KLAS 2025 found +$13,049/year average across sites (primarily from HCC capture).

Units in this Algorithm
Primary

Secondary

⚠ Use the same prior-year period as the pre-adoption window to control for seasonal variation in encounter mix and wRVU output.

Pseudocode

The value Suki provides to this measure is the adoption boundary (first session date), the active usage flag (monthly denominator), and the session timestamp (optional within-week active-use period definition). These inputs are necessary to construct the pre/post comparison but do not constitute the outcome signal.

CM-21 — Coding Accuracy

NB — Net Benefits Coding Accuracy Operationalizations: ICD-10 Coding Depth  ·  ICD-10 Suggestion Match Rate  ·  Denial Rate

Measure Overview

Construct
Documentation-driven coding quality — three operationalizations
↑ Higher is better (1, 2) ↓ Lower is better (3)

Coding accuracy captures whether clinical documentation supports correct, complete coding of the patient encounter. Suki adoption may improve coding by generating more complete transcripts that surface diagnoses and complexity levels a provider might otherwise omit when documenting under time pressure.

The three operationalizations measure different facets of coding quality: depth (how many codes per encounter), accuracy (how well Suki’s suggestions match what was billed), and payer validation (whether higher coding produces more denials). Algorithm 2 is the only one where Suki contributes primary data.

Three Operationalizations — See Algorithms Below
1ICD-10 diagnosis codes per encounter  —  proxy for documentation completeness  ↑ Higher is better
2ICD-10 suggestion match rate (%)  —  Suki-native accuracy signal  ↑ Higher is better
3Level 4–5 denial rate (%)  —  payer validation of coding uplift  ↓ Lower is better
⚠ Cross-Cutting Ambiguities
  • More codes ≠ better coding. ICD-10 coding depth (Algorithm 1) is a completeness proxy, not a direct accuracy measure. Very high code counts can signal overcoding. Always read alongside denial rate (Algorithm 3) to confirm additional codes are accepted by payers.
  • Case mix confounding. A shift toward more complex patients will raise both ICD-10 code counts and E/M levels independently of Suki. Use within-provider pre/post comparisons and flag known panel changes during the analysis window.
  • Algorithm 2 requires a pipeline not in the standard data pull. Comparing Suki’s suggested codes to final billed codes requires encounter-to-claim linkage. Suki may also return IMO codes that need mapping to ICD-10 before comparison. Confirm pipeline availability before planning work that depends on Algorithm 2.
  • Denial reason codes are required for Algorithm 3. Not all denials are coding-related. Distinguish coding denials from authorization and eligibility denials using denial reason codes. A rising overall denial rate driven by authorization changes is not a Suki signal.

Algorithms

1 ICD-10 Coding Depth ↑ Higher is better Diagnoses per encounter

Counts the average number of distinct ICD-10 diagnosis codes assigned per completed encounter. 2 ICD-10 Suggestion Match Rate ↑ Higher is better Suki Native Data Suki-to-billed code agreement

The only CM-21 algorithm where Suki contributes the primary signal. Compares AI-generated ICD-10 codes from the Suki structured-data endpoint to final billed codes from the EHR claim record. A higher match rate indicates Suki’s coding suggestions are accurate and align with what clinicians ultimately bill. Afshar 2025b (RCT, n=66) found statistically significant coding improvement (p < 0.001). A mature implementation should approach 85–90% match rate.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
ICD-10 suggestion match rate (%)
For each completed encounter, count the Suki-suggested ICD-10 codes that also appear in the final billed codes. Divide by total billed codes.

Secondary

3 Denial Rate — Level 4–5 Claims ↓ Lower is better Payer validation of coding uplift

The denial rate for Level 4–5 claims serves as a payer-side validity check on coding improvement. When E/M levels and ICD-10 code counts rise after Suki adoption, a stable or declining denial rate confirms that the uplift reflects legitimate documentation-supported complexity — not overcoding. Holmgren 2026 found no increase in denials despite coding improvement, a meaningful null result. A typical Level 4–5 denial baseline is under 3%.

⚠ Distinguish coding-related denials from authorization and eligibility denials using denial reason codes. Only coding-related denials are relevant to this measure.

This metric is a guard rail, not a standalone outcome. Read it in parallel with Algorithm 3 of CM-20 (E/M Level Improvement). A rising L4–5 share + stable denial rate = legitimate coding uplift. A rising L4–5 share + rising denial rate = possible overcoding.

Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-22 — Patient Volume & Throughput

NB — Net Benefits Patient Volume Also known as: Encounters per Provider per Week  ·  Patient Throughput  ·  Volume Capacity

Unit

Unit
Completed encounters per provider per week
↑ Higher is better

A completed encounter is a patient visit that has a signed clinical note in the EHR. Encounters are counted per provider per clinical week and normalized to each provider's scheduled clinical hours to account for part-time work. The metric captures whether providers can see more patients as documentation burden decreases after Suki adoption.

Gold Standard
Completed, signed encounters from the EHR, normalized to scheduled clinical hours per week. Preferred — captures realized patient throughput and controls for part-time schedules.
Default / Practical
Scheduled encounters per week (from the EHR schedule), when completion and signing status are unavailable. Use with caution — scheduled volume does not equal completed volume and may not reflect the documentation-driven capacity effect.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Normalize for scheduled clinical hours. A provider who reduces from 5 to 4 clinical days will show a raw encounter drop unrelated to Suki. Divide by scheduled hours or clinical days to compute a rate; compare rates, not raw counts.
  • Exclude providers who changed practice context. Providers who changed specialty, site, or panel size during the analysis window introduce confounders that are unrelated to Suki adoption. Exclude these providers or analyze them separately.
  • Only include completed, signed encounters. Scheduled-but-not-completed visits (no-shows, cancellations) and encounters without a signed note should be excluded. Mixing completion statuses inflates raw counts and obscures the capacity signal.
Interpretation caution: Increased encounter volume is not universally desirable. Higher throughput achieved through documentation efficiency gains is a positive signal, but it should be interpreted alongside workload indicators and care quality measures. A provider seeing more patients without a corresponding reduction in documentation time (CM-04) warrants investigation.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki identifies which weeks are active-use weeks, but encounter counts come entirely from the EHR. The Suki session count per week provides a finer-grained signal than the adoption date alone: a provider who used Suki in 3 out of 4 weeks in a month can be analyzed at the week level, with the 3 Suki weeks as “post-adoption” and the 1 non-Suki week as a within-provider control.

This week-level design is stronger than a simple pre/post month comparison for short analysis windows. It requires Suki session data at the weekly grain and matched encounter data from the EHR at the same grain.
