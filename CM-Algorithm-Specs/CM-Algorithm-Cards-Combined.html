CM-01 — Clinician Burnout and Exhaustion

NB — Net Benefits Provider Wellbeing Also known as: Work Exhaustion  ·  Emotional Exhaustion  ·  Burnout Syndrome

Unit

Unit
PFI Work Exhaustion subscale score (1–5)
↓ Lower is better

Provider-reported emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and interpersonal disengagement from clinical work — the core burnout syndrome — measured via a validated psychometric instrument administered at defined intervals around Suki adoption. Distinct from in-encounter cognitive load (CM-02) and professional fulfillment (CM-03), which are adjacent but dissociable constructs.

Gold Standard
Stanford Professional Fulfillment Index (PFI), Work Exhaustion subscale — 10-item, 1–5 scale (higher = worse). The instrument used in the strongest external RCT (Afshar 2025b, n=66), which found a −0.44 reduction.
Default / Practical
Mini-Z / Mini-Z 2.0 — 10-item brief burnout screen, 10–50 scale (higher = better, opposite polarity from PFI). Lower respondent burden; widely used in quality-improvement contexts and proposed as the Phase 0/Phase 3 instrument.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • The baseline problem. A true pre/post comparison requires a baseline survey administered before Suki go-live. If Suki is engaged retrospectively, this window is permanently lost — CM-01 then can only be measured as a cohort-level dose-response comparison (heavy vs. light users at follow-up), not an individual-level pre/post change.
  • Self-report inflation. Self-report-only studies (Shah 2024: −1.94 on the PFI Work Exhaustion subscale) substantially overstate effect size relative to the RCT (Afshar 2025b: −0.44) — a gap expected from volunteer/selection bias in non-randomized designs. Do not present self-report-only deltas as equivalent to RCT effect sizes.
  • Instrument-direction mismatch. PFI Work Exhaustion is 1–5 where higher = worse; Mini-Z is 10–50 where higher = better. If a site switches instruments between baseline and follow-up, scores must be normalized to a common direction before any comparison is made.
  • Individual-level linkage vs. anonymity. Linking a provider's baseline survey response to their post-deployment utilization tier requires either non-anonymous administration (reduces candor on a sensitive topic) or a study-specific pseudonymous code maintained outside Suki.

Algorithm

Produces a cohort-level burnout score for each administration wave, compared across Suki utilization tiers rather than (or in addition to) a simple pre/post pooled comparison. The score is expected to decrease (PFI scale) after Suki adoption, with the largest decrease in the heaviest-utilization cohort.

CM-02 — Cognitive and Task Load

NB — Net Benefits Provider Wellbeing Also known as: Mental Workload  ·  NASA-TLX  ·  Physician Task Load

Unit

Unit
NASA-TLX / PTL score (0–100)
↓ Lower is better

The mental effort, time pressure, and frustration a clinician experiences during or immediately after a clinical encounter — the in-the-moment cognitive demand of documentation, measured via the NASA Task Load Index or a brief physician-specific adaptation. Distinct from CM-01 (sustained emotional exhaustion over weeks-to-months) and CM-04 (objective documentation duration).

Gold Standard
NASA-TLX (full) — 6-domain instrument (Mental Demand, Physical Demand, Temporal Demand, Performance, Effort, Frustration), 0–100 per subscale. Used in Bracken 2025, PHTI 2025, Omon 2025, Stults 2025. Frustration and Temporal Demand subscales are most relevant to documentation-specific burden.
Default / Practical
Physician Task Load Index (PTL) — 4-item NASA-TLX adaptation, 0–100 scale (lower = better), used in Shah 2024. Lower respondent burden, suitable for repeated or frequent administration (e.g., end-of-shift).
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • The timing problem. NASA-TLX validity depends on proximity to the encounter — ideally administered immediately post-encounter, not weeks later. Suki has no mechanism to trigger a micro-survey on session completion, so administration falls back to end-of-shift or periodic, which introduces recall aggregation across multiple encounters.
  • Composite vs. subscale. Full NASA-TLX (6 domains) vs. Frustration/Temporal Demand subscales only vs. PTL (4 items) are not interchangeable scores. Pick one operationalization and apply it consistently across the comparison window.
  • Pathway attribution. Four distinct hooks (H1 ambient documentation, H8 orders staging, H10 pre-visit summaries, H11 chart Q&A) plausibly reduce cognitive load through different mechanisms. A NASA-TLX change cannot, by itself, identify which pathway drove it — pathway isolation requires session-level exposure flags not yet available.

The score is expected to be lower (PTL/NASA-TLX scale) in heavier-utilization cohorts.

CM-03 — Professional Fulfillment and Meaningfulness

NB — Net Benefits Provider Wellbeing Secondary / Exploratory Also known as: Meaning in Work  ·  PFI Professional Fulfillment Subscale

Unit

Unit
PFI Professional Fulfillment subscale score (1–5)
↑ Higher is better

Provider-reported positive engagement with clinical work — the presence of meaning, reward, and satisfaction in the work itself. This is the positive pole of the wellbeing spectrum, dissociable from CM-01 (absence of exhaustion). Removing a negative (burnout) does not imply adding a positive (fulfillment), and the strongest available evidence shows exactly this dissociation.

Gold Standard
Stanford Professional Fulfillment Index (PFI), Professional Fulfillment subscale — 6-item, 1–5 scale (higher = better). The instrument used in Afshar 2025b. If a full PFI is administered for CM-01, this subscale is collected "for free" in the same instrument.
Default / Practical
Custom meaningfulness-of-work and work-life balance Likert items (Afshar 2025b, Boyter 2025, Duggan 2025) — conceptually adjacent supplements with weaker instrument validation; not a substitute for the PFI subscale.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Expected null result. Afshar 2025b (RCT, n=66) found professional fulfillment increased at p=0.04, which missed the pre-specified alpha of 0.025, while burnout (CM-01) decreased significantly (−0.44). The expected outcome for CM-03 is a null result — this should be stated up front in any report, not discovered after the fact.
  • Low actionability. The H1 deep-dive scores CM-03 as Tier 3 (7/12), driven by Low Actionability — no current Suki hook is designed to act on meaning, reward, or engagement (unlike CM-01/CM-02, where H1/H8/H10/H11 plausibly act on burden or cognitive-load mechanisms). Even a clean measurement gives no lever to pull.
  • Do not separately scope. If a full Stanford PFI is administered for CM-01, the Professional Fulfillment subscale is collected in the same instrument at no additional respondent burden. Standing up a separate CM-03 data-collection effort is not recommended.
  • Framing risk. Reporting CM-03 as a standalone, primary value-proposition metric risks being read as "Suki doesn't improve wellbeing" when the more accurate reading is "Suki improves one wellbeing construct (burnout) and not a different, dissociable one (fulfillment)."

The expected result is a flat or non-significant change in this score even where CM-01's burnout score decreases.

CM-04 — Documentation Time

NB — Net Benefits Documentation Efficiency Also known as: Time in Notes  Â·  Note Completion Time  Â·  Documentation Duration

Unit

Unit
Documentation minutes per encounter
↓ Lower is better

A documentation minute is any minute of activity attributable to note creation or editing for a given encounter — counted from encounter open through final note signature, including post-signature addenda. Measured via EHR audit-log telemetry (preferred) or Suki session duration (fallback). Each minute is attributed to the encounter in which the documentation occurred.

Gold Standard
EHR audit-log telemetry (e.g., Epic Signal / UAL) capturing time-stamped, per-user documentation activity bounded to note creation and editing. Preferred — objective and captures the full note-to-sign lifecycle inside the EHR.
Default / Practical
Suki session duration: start-to-end timestamp per dictation or ambient capture session. Use when EHR audit-log data are unavailable. Captures in-app documentation time only; misses editing and signing that occurs inside the EHR after the Suki session ends.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Per-encounter vs. per-note vs. per-day. Documentation time is reported per encounter, per note, or per provider per day across the literature. These units are not interchangeable. Choose one and apply it consistently across the pre- and post-adoption windows.
  • Self-report vs. objective telemetry. Self-reported documentation time overestimates improvement by 3–6× relative to EHR audit-log data (Ma 2025; PHTI 2025). Do not mix self-report and objective sources in the same analysis.
  • What counts as documentation time. Some EHR telemetry fields include inbox, chart review, or order entry in their “documentation time” metric. Confirm whether your EHR field is scoped to note creation and editing only, or to broader EHR activity. The latter overlaps with CM-07 (Total EHR Time).

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki session timestamps are the strongest Suki-native signal for this measure — session start and end times are recorded for every dictation and ambient capture session and are available directly from the Suki API. For organizations without EHR audit-log access, Suki session duration is a usable fallback proxy for in-app documentation time.

For the most accurate measure of total documentation time per encounter, use EHR audit-log telemetry; reserve Suki session duration as a fallback only.

CM-06 — Chart Closure Timeliness

Unit

Unit

↑ Higher is better

Whether and how quickly clinical notes are completed and signed — a documentation-completion-behavior measure, distinct from how long the note took to write (CM-04). A note can be drafted in 3 minutes (fast CM-04) and sit unsigned for 2 days (poor CM-06), or take 15 minutes to draft but be signed immediately. CM-09 — Note Inaccuracy Rate

IQ — Information Quality Pilot / Calibration Design Also known as: Hallucination Rate  ·  Addition Errors  ·  Negation Errors  ·  Freedom from Hallucination (PDQI)

Unit

Unit
% of notes containing ≥1 fabricated or incorrect element
↓ Lower is better

The presence of clinically inaccurate or fabricated content in an AI-generated note — information that was not stated in the encounter, or stated but factually wrong. Distinct from CM-08 (Note Completeness), which measures what was left out. Hallucinations and incorrect facts are less frequent than omissions but more dangerous per event: a note that says nothing about chest pain is an omission; a note that inverts a negation ("denies chest pain" → "reports chest pain") is a patient safety risk.

Gold Standard
PDQI-9 / PDSQI-9 Accuracy domain — 5-point Likert subscale including a falsification/fabrication criterion, scored by physician raters. PDSQI-9 is the AI-note-adapted version; Afshar 2025b validated an LLM-as-judge implementation against physician ratings with agreement sufficient for research-grade use.
Default / Practical
Automated transcript-to-note flagging using Suki's source_transcripts[] evidence-linking primitive — per-sentence hallucination / addition / negation-reversal flags, used as a pre-screening signal and sampling frame for the human/LLM rating pipeline below.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • The Missing Feedback Loop. Suki generates a note; the physician reviews, edits, and signs it in the EHR. The physician's edits are the most direct signal of where Suki was wrong — but Suki never sees those edits. Without the EHR's final note text and edit/version history, CM-09 can only be measured against the transcript, not the clinician's final intent, and only via one-off rater exercises rather than continuous monitoring.
  • Wide published-rate range (3–36%). Baker 2023 (36%), Palm 2025 (31%), Kernberg 2023 (3.2% incorrect facts but 86% omissions) — rates vary enormously by error definition (hallucination vs. incorrect-fact vs. negation-reversal vs. any flagged sentence). The error taxonomy must be fixed before any rate is reported or compared across sources.
  • Reproducibility / cross-replicate consistency. Kernberg 2023 found only 53% of data elements consistent across 3 model runs of the same case — different errors appear on each run, so a single-pass audit may not represent the model's typical behavior. Consider whether a pilot should sample multiple generations per encounter.
  • Note Amendment Rate (CM-08/CM-09 boundary). The corpus flags an unresolved "row-13 amendment rate" item — post-signature re-opens that correct wrong content belong to CM-09; re-opens that add missing content belong to CM-08. Clarify with Suki whether their amendment-rate concept distinguishes these, or covers any pre-signature edit.

Unit

Unit
PDRQ-9 score (9-item, 1–5)
↑ Higher is better

The patient's own report of the quality of the patient-physician interaction — relationship quality, satisfaction with the visit, and the experience of being heard. This is the most underdeveloped outcome domain in the entire canonical measures corpus: only Owens 2024 has used a validated patient-reported instrument, and it found a null result in the masked phase (p=.31) — the open-label phase was positive, more consistent with a patient-expectation effect than a true relationship-quality effect.

Patient harm outcomes associated with ambient AI documentation — adverse events, AHRQ harm scores, and gaps between what gets documented and what clinical action actually follows. ⚠ Scope Narrowing (Critical — Read Before Reporting Any Error-Rate Data)
  • True patient safety requires measured patient harm outcomes. Errors in documentation that do not demonstrate a downstream patient outcome are technical quality issues (→ CM-09 Note Inaccuracy Rate), not safety. Severity of error is not patient safety without evidence of patient harm.
  • Several commonly-cited "safety" items are likely misclassified absent outcome evidence: "Clinically Significant Errors in AI Notes" (Biro 2025, Anderson 2025) → CM-09; "Negation Errors" (Bundy 2024) → CM-09; RDoC/PHQ-9 documentation level (Castro 2025) → CM-08; "Likelihood of Psychiatric Intervention, aOR" (Castro 2025) → quality-of-care/clinical-decision-rate, adjacent to but not itself safety.
  • Suki's own evidence-linking and note-accuracy work (CM-09's H15/source_transcripts[]) will generate data that looks safety-relevant but should be reported under CM-09/CM-08 unless paired with an observed clinical action or harm signal.
  • H8 and H11 are the relevant future hooks for CM-19, not H1. Any CM-19 evaluation plan should be scoped against H8/H11 deployment timelines, not current production capability.
  • Pearlman/Castro-style aOR findings cut both directions. A significant aOR (e.g., Castro 2025's 0.83, p=.005 — more documentation, less intervention) is a potential unintended consequence, not automatically "bad" without further investigation — but it is the kind of signal CM-19 needs.
