Epic-Sourced Canonical Measures

Regenstrief Institute / Indiana University — algorithm specifications for the Suki AI canonical measures that can be evaluated directly from a health system’s own Epic instance.

Scope of This Document

Suki AI’s canonical measure set spans clinician well-being, note quality, adoption, financial productivity, and EHR-efficiency constructs. Of these, five — Documentation Time (CM-04), After-Hours Documentation (CM-05), Chart Closure Timeliness (CM-06), Total EHR Time (CM-07), and Patient Volume & Throughput (CM-22) — have a plausible data source in Epic’s Clarity data model. This document covers those five measures only.

The remaining canonical measures — survey-based well-being and satisfaction measures, note-quality ratings, financial/productivity measures, and evaluation-methodology measures — rely on survey instruments, human/LLM rating, or claims data rather than Epic Clarity, and are out of scope for this deliverable.

Pre-Work Already Underway

Ahead of formally kicking off Sprint 2, we’re confirming feasibility across all five of these measures — not just Documentation Time, which is furthest along — by looking at what test queries against a live Epic instance actually return, and checking whether the underlying Clarity tables have been populated consistently since go-live (a gap there is a red flag, not an expected pattern). The goal is to catch weak-data risk now, so Sprint 2 isn’t the first place we discover it.

  2. Shop candidate algorithms to other health systems

    Package one or more of these Epic-sourced measure specifications so additional health systems can evaluate feasibility against their own Epic instance — a narrow, low-effort ask (run a query, share what came back) rather than a full study commitment.

  3. Work with Suki’s technical team on a repeatable process

    Collaborate with Suki’s engineering team to turn the field-mapping-and-validation workflow into a documented, repeatable process — potentially built into Suki’s own tooling — so new measures or new health systems can be onboarded without a bespoke one-off analysis each time.

  4. Evaluate additional candidate measures

    A few other canonical measures came up on the call as possibly having an Epic source but weren’t confirmed — for example, Coding Accuracy (CM-21), where E/M visit-level and HCC diagnosis codes may or may not exist in Clarity. Worth a quick check alongside the Sprint 2 field-mapping work before deciding whether to expand this set.

Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-04 — Documentation Time

NB — Net Benefits Documentation Efficiency Also known as: Time in Notes  ·  Note Completion Time  ·  Documentation Duration

Unit

Unit
Documentation minutes per encounter
↓ Lower is better

A documentation minute is any minute of activity attributable to note creation or editing for a given encounter — counted from encounter open through final note signature, including post-signature addenda. Measured via EHR audit-log telemetry (preferred) or Suki session duration (fallback). Each minute is attributed to the encounter in which the documentation occurred.

Gold Standard
EHR audit-log telemetry (e.g., Epic Signal / UAL) capturing time-stamped, per-user documentation activity bounded to note creation and editing. Preferred — objective and captures the full note-to-sign lifecycle inside the EHR.
Default / Practical
Suki session duration: start-to-end timestamp per dictation or ambient capture session. Use when EHR audit-log data are unavailable. Captures in-app documentation time only; misses editing and signing that occurs inside the EHR after the Suki session ends.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Per-encounter vs. per-note vs. per-day. Documentation time is reported per encounter, per note, or per provider per day across the literature. These units are not interchangeable. Choose one and apply it consistently across the pre- and post-adoption windows.
  • Self-report vs. objective telemetry. Self-reported documentation time overestimates improvement by 3–6× relative to EHR audit-log data (Ma 2025; PHTI 2025). Do not mix self-report and objective sources in the same analysis.
  • What counts as documentation time. Some EHR telemetry fields include inbox, chart review, or order entry in their “documentation time” metric. Confirm whether your EHR field is scoped to note creation and editing only, or to broader EHR activity. The latter overlaps with CM-07 (Total EHR Time).

Algorithm

Produces the average documentation minutes per encounter per provider in the cohort for a given month. This number is expected to decrease after Suki adoption as note creation becomes faster and requires less time per visit.

When Epic Signal/UAL telemetry is unavailable, per-note authoring time can be approximated from Clarity’s note edit-event log. This is a within-provider proxy — directionally valid for pre/post Suki comparisons, but not a substitute for Signal minutes. Two tiers are available, in priority order.

Epic Clarity Tables — Join path verified in EHI knowledge graph
Required
NOTES_HISTORY_LOG
Per-edit event stream: timestamps (EDIT_HX_INSTANT) for note creation, edits, and signature events. Drives the sessionization in Tier 1.
⚠ If this table only records create and sign events (not intermediate edits), Tier 1 degenerates to Tier 2 — verify event density against real data before choosing the tier.
Required
HNO_INFO
Note master: ENTRY_USER_ID (note author), PAT_ENC_CSN_ID (encounter link), CREATE_INSTANT_DTTM and LST_FILED_INST_DTTM (create and sign timestamps), UNSIGNED_YN (signature status).
⚠ Attribute documentation time to ENTRY_USER_ID (note author), not VISIT_PROV_ID from PAT_ENC — they differ for scribe-entered notes and resident/attending co-sign workflows.
Filter
PAT_ENC
Encounter-level filters: ENC_TYPE_C (exclude telephone/proxy types), EFFECTIVE_DATE_DTTM, and the join to per-provider Suki adoption data.

Tier 1 (Preferred) — Sessionized Active Authoring Time
Collapse a note’s edit events into work sessions (gap threshold: 10 min default, tunable). Sum only within-session durations; discard idle gaps. Output: average active authoring minutes per note per provider.

Pseudocode — Tier 1 (sessionized)
-- 1. per-note edit events attributed to the note author WITH ev AS ( SELECT h.NOTE_ID, h.ENTRY_USER_ID AS author_id, h.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID, l.EDIT_HX_INSTANT AS ts FROM HNO_INFO h JOIN NOTES_HISTORY_LOG l ON l.NOTE_ID = h.NOTE_ID WHERE h.UNSIGNED_YN = 'N' -- signed notes only ), -- 2. gap to previous edit; gap > 10 min = new session gaps AS ( SELECT *, DATEDIFF(minute, LAG(ts) OVER (PARTITION BY NOTE_ID ORDER BY ts), ts) AS gap_min FROM ev ), seg AS ( SELECT NOTE_ID, author_id, PAT_ENC_CSN_ID, SUM(CASE WHEN gap_min IS NULL OR gap_min > 10 THEN 0 ELSE gap_min END) AS active_min -- ⚠ 10-min threshold is tunable FROM gaps GROUP BY NOTE_ID, author_id, PAT_ENC_CSN_ID ) SELECT s.author_id, phase(e.EFFECTIVE_DATE_DTTM, k.suki_golive) AS phase, -- ⚠ confirm Suki go-live AVG(s.active_min) AS avg_active_doc_min_per_note FROM seg s JOIN PAT_ENC e ON e.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID = s.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID JOIN suki_adoption k ON k.epic_prov_id = s.author_id WHERE e.ENC_CLOSED_YN = 'Y' AND e.ENC_TYPE_C NOT IN (1, 31) GROUP BY s.author_id, phase;

Tier 2 (Fallback) — Create→Sign Elapsed Span
Single subtraction per note: wall-clock time from CREATE_INSTANT_DTTM to LST_FILED_INST_DTTM. Simpler but includes idle periods — overstates active work.

Pseudocode — Tier 2 (elapsed span)
SELECT h.ENTRY_USER_ID AS author_id, AVG(DATEDIFF(minute, h.CREATE_INSTANT_DTTM, h.LST_FILED_INST_DTTM)) AS avg_span_min FROM HNO_INFO h WHERE h.UNSIGNED_YN = 'N' GROUP BY h.ENTRY_USER_ID; -- Wall-clock; includes idle time → overstates active authoring
⚠ Proxy Caveats & Guardrails
  • Event-gated, not keystroke-gated. This measures wall-clock between logged edit events, not keystroke-active time. Tier 1 is less sensitive to long idle gaps than Tier 2, but is still a proxy. Report it as “note-authoring time,” not “documentation time.”
  • Floor estimate, not full Signal. Signal/UAL captures note authoring, chart review, inbox, and order entry. This proxy captures only note-authoring events — a subset. Do not compare proxy minutes directly to Signal minutes without a correction factor.
  • Calibrate against Signal where possible. If any provider-period overlap between Signal and Clarity data exists, compute a correction factor rather than presenting proxy minutes as equivalent.
  • Multi-author notes. Attribution uses ENTRY_USER_ID. Resident or scribe edits on an attending’s note should not be charged to the attending.
  • Open questions before deployment: (1) What is the actual event density in NOTES_HISTORY_LOG — does it log intermediate edits or only create/sign endpoints? (2) Is any Signal extract available for calibration on a provider subset? (3) What session threshold (5, 10, or 15 min) best fits local workflows?
Expected Trend — Documentation Min / Encounter / Month

Cohort average should decrease after Suki adoption. The pre-adoption window establishes the baseline; improvement is expected to compound as Suki utilization matures beyond the onboarding ramp. Literature benchmarks: −12% to −29% (EHR telemetry, Ma 2025; Guo 2026); −25% to −72% (self-report, AAFP 2021 — objective preferred).

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki session timestamps are the strongest Suki-native signal for this measure — session start and end times are recorded for every dictation and ambient capture session and are available directly from the Suki API. For organizations without EHR audit-log access, Suki session duration is a usable fallback proxy for in-app documentation time.

However, Suki session time does not capture the full documentation lifecycle. Note editing, formatting, and signing that occur inside the EHR after a Suki session closes are invisible to Suki. For the most accurate measure of total documentation time per encounter, use EHR audit-log telemetry; reserve Suki session duration as a fallback only.

CM-05 — After-Hours Documentation

NB — Net Benefits Documentation Efficiency Also known as: Pajama Time  ·  Work Outside Work  ·  After-Hours EHR Time

Unit

Unit
After-hours documentation minutes
↓ Lower is better

A documentation minute is after-hours if it falls outside the provider's working hours on that calendar day. Minutes are counted from encounter open through final note signature, including any post-signature addenda and edits.

Gold Standard
Per-provider or per-specialty working hours, as configured by the care setting. Preferred — avoids systematic misclassification across specialties with non-standard hours.
Default / Practical
Before 09:00 or after 17:00 on weekdays; any time on Saturday or Sunday. Use when per-provider scheduling data are unavailable.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Working hours vary by specialty. Surgeons may work 09:00–21:00; a 9–5 default over-counts after-hours time for them and cannot be compared across specialties without adjustment.
  • When does documentation end? We recommend counting through final signature and all addenda; stopping at initial note closure understates after-hours activity and is not directly comparable across implementations.
  • Pre-encounter prep vs. after-hours. Early chart review (e.g., 06:00 before rounds) may be classified as after-hours under a fixed window even within a provider's normal practice. Provider-level scheduling resolves this; the 9–5 default does not.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's contribution to this measure is the adoption boundary, not the documentation signal. Suki session data indicates when a dictation or ambient capture session was initiated, but it does not measure total EHR documentation time. Work within the EHR before or after a Suki session is invisible to Suki; note signing happens in the EHR, not in Suki.

The authoritative data source is the EHR audit log. Using Suki session timing as a proxy for after-hours documentation time would systematically undercount documentation minutes and miss chart review, note editing, and order entry that occurs outside any Suki session.

CM-06 — Chart Closure Timeliness

EFF — Documentation Efficiency Tier 1 — 10/12 Also known as: Same-Day Encounter Closure  ·  Chart Closure Time  ·  24-Hour Documentation Deficiency Rate  ·  Pre-Next-Visit Closure

Unit

Unit
% of encounters with notes closed within 24 hours
↑ Higher is better

Whether and how quickly clinical notes are completed and signed — a documentation-completion-behavior measure, distinct from how long the note took to write (CM-04). A note can be drafted in 3 minutes (fast CM-04) and sit unsigned for 2 days (poor CM-06), or take 15 minutes to draft but be signed immediately. The canonical definition prefers the 24-hour, duration-bounded window for pre/post evaluation over the stricter calendar-bounded "same-day" convention.

Gold Standard
EHR-confirmed note-signature timestamp vs. encounter-end timestamp, reported as a 24-hour closure rate (%) or 24-hour deficiency rate (% — the complement). This is the basis for Boyter 2025/KLAS's −41% chart-closure-time finding (UAL data) and FMOL's 84% improvement in notes closed within 7 days.
Default / Practical
Suki draft-availability rate within 24 hours — using the elapsed time between the session-completion timestamp (status = completed) and the encounter-end timestamp, bucketed the same way as the EHR-confirmed measure. A leading proxy, not the canonical measure itself (see ambiguity below).
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Same-day vs. 24-hour window. "Same-day" is calendar-bounded — a 4:45 PM encounter closed at 7:00 AM the next day fails "same-day" despite closing in under 15 hours. "Within 24 hours" is duration-bounded and does not penalize late-day visits. The canonical definition recommends the 24-hour window for pre/post evaluation; document which convention any given site uses, since Suki's own bucket and an EHR-reported "same-day" rate will diverge for reasons unrelated to documentation behavior.
  • Overlap with CM-04. The "time to chart" framing risks restating CM-04 (Documentation Time). CM-06's scope is held strictly to completion/signature behavior, not drafting duration — even though both measures draw on the same underlying Suki session-timestamp data.
  • Draft-availability vs. signature. Suki's status = completed event marks the note draft as ready, not signed. The clinician still has to review and sign it in the EHR. Any Suki-only report of "closure" should be explicitly labeled draft-availability until EHR signature data is confirmed available.
  • Bucket field source-of-truth unconfirmed. The Suki-EHR crosswalk already defines same_day_count, next_day_count, two_three_day_count, four_five_day_count, six_plus_day_count — but whether their "day signed" component is EHR-sourced (answers CM-06 directly) or derived from Suki's own completion timestamp (answers draft-availability) is unconfirmed.
  • Pearlman 2025 benchmark unverified. Its "Time to Close Encounter" outcome is not yet confirmed to measure note signature vs. a broader workflow event (e.g., room turnover, discharge) — treat as a candidate, not confirmed, benchmark.

Algorithm

For each completed session, computes the elapsed time between the encounter-end timestamp and the session-completion timestamp, assigns it to a closure-delay bucket (same-day / next-day / 2–3 days / 4–5 days / 6+ days — mirroring the bucket structure already defined in the Suki-EHR crosswalk), and reports the cohort-level 24-hour draft-availability rate plus the full bucket distribution.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
24-hour draft-availability rate (%)
Share of completed sessions where (session-completion timestamp − encounter-end timestamp) ≤ 24 hours. The canonical-unit analog of the EHR-confirmed 24-hour closure rate, computed from Suki-native timestamps.
⚠ Pipeline gap: this measures when the draft became available, not when the note was signed. Without an EHR signature-confirmation callback or export, this rate is a leading-indicator proxy, not the canonical measure — the single biggest structural gap for CM-06.
Secondary
Closure-delay bucket distribution
Same-day / next-day / 2–3 days / 4–5 days / 6+ days — the full distribution of draft-availability delay, reported as counts mirroring same_day_count ... six_plus_day_count in the crosswalk. Reported alongside the primary rate to show the shape of the tail, not just the 24-hour cutoff.
⚠ If/when these crosswalk fields are confirmed to be EHR-signature-sourced for a given site, they should be used directly in place of this Suki-derived bucketing — the formula below would then need no Suki-side timestamp math at all.
Secondary
Session status distribution (denominator hygiene)
completed vs. skipped/failed/aborted — defines the denominator correctly, excluding encounters where no Suki draft was ever produced rather than counting them as "never closed."
24-Hour Draft-Availability Rate=COUNT(sessions where delay_hours ≤ 24)COUNT(sessions where status = completed) × 100
delay_hours = session_completion_timestamp − encounter_end_timestamp (hours) Bucket assignment (mirrors crosswalk fields): same_day_count : delay_hours ≤ 24 AND signed same calendar day as encounter next_day_count : delay_hours ≤ 24 AND signed next calendar day (or 24 < delay_hours ≤ 48) two_three_day_count : 48 < delay_hours ≤ 72 four_five_day_count : 72 < delay_hours ≤ 120 six_plus_day_count : delay_hours > 120 Denominator excludes skipped/failed/aborted sessions (no draft ever produced) Report: 24-hour rate (primary) + full bucket distribution (secondary), per cohort and pre/post adoption
Pseudocode

CM-07 — Total EHR Time

NB — Net Benefits EHR Efficiency Also known as: Total EHR Time  ·  Login Time  ·  Active EHR Time  ·  EHR Burden

Unit

Unit
Total EHR minutes per encounter
↓ Lower is better

A total EHR minute is any minute of logged activity in the EHR system by a given provider, across all task types — note creation, inbox management, order entry, results review, and any other EHR interaction. Minutes are summed per provider per encounter-equivalent period and normalized to completed encounters.

Gold Standard
Full EHR audit-log telemetry capturing all activity types across all EHR modules (documentation, inbox, orders, results, admin). Preferred — gives the broadest and most complete view of EHR burden.
Default / Practical
Documentation time + inbox time only, when full audit-log is unavailable. These two components are the most reliably extracted from most EHR telemetry systems and account for the largest share of total EHR time.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Scope of "EHR time." Different EHR telemetry fields vary in which activities they include. Before using any field, confirm whether it captures documentation only, documentation + inbox, or all EHR activity. Mixing narrow and broad definitions across pre/post windows invalidates the comparison. This measure is explicitly broader than CM-04 (Documentation Time).
  • Per-encounter vs. per-workday normalization. Some studies report total EHR time per day (Ma 2025: −19.95 min/day); others normalize to encounters. Per-encounter normalization is preferred when encounter volume itself may change after Suki adoption (see CM-22). Per-day is acceptable when encounter volume is stable.
  • Inbox and orders may dilute the Suki signal. Suki directly impacts documentation time; its effect on inbox and order entry is indirect and smaller. A large inbox component in the denominator will attenuate the observed effect size relative to a documentation-only measure. Report the component breakdown when possible.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki provides a partial proxy for the documentation component of total EHR time, but cannot observe inbox activity, order entry, or results review. CM-22 — Patient Volume & Throughput

NB — Net Benefits Patient Volume Also known as: Encounters per Provider per Week  ·  Patient Throughput  ·  Volume Capacity

Unit

Unit
Completed encounters per provider per week
↑ Higher is better

A completed encounter is a patient visit that has a signed clinical note in the EHR. Encounters are counted per provider per clinical week and normalized to each provider's scheduled clinical hours to account for part-time work. The metric captures whether providers can see more patients as documentation burden decreases after Suki adoption.

Gold Standard
Completed, signed encounters from the EHR, normalized to scheduled clinical hours per week. Preferred — captures realized patient throughput and controls for part-time schedules.
Default / Practical
Scheduled encounters per week (from the EHR schedule), when completion and signing status are unavailable. Use with caution — scheduled volume does not equal completed volume and may not reflect the documentation-driven capacity effect.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Normalize for scheduled clinical hours. A provider who reduces from 5 to 4 clinical days will show a raw encounter drop unrelated to Suki. Divide by scheduled hours or clinical days to compute a rate; compare rates, not raw counts.
  • Exclude providers who changed practice context. Providers who changed specialty, site, or panel size during the analysis window introduce confounders that are unrelated to Suki adoption. Exclude these providers or analyze them separately.
  • Only include completed, signed encounters. Scheduled-but-not-completed visits (no-shows, cancellations) and encounters without a signed note should be excluded. Mixing completion statuses inflates raw counts and obscures the capacity signal.
Interpretation caution: Increased encounter volume is not universally desirable. Higher throughput achieved through documentation efficiency gains is a positive signal, but it should be interpreted alongside workload indicators and care quality measures. A provider seeing more patients without a corresponding reduction in documentation time (CM-04) warrants investigation.
