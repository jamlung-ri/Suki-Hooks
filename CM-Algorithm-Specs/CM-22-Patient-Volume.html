Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-22 — Patient Volume & Throughput

NB — Net Benefits Patient Volume Also known as: Encounters per Provider per Week  ·  Patient Throughput  ·  Volume Capacity

Unit

Unit
Completed encounters per provider per week
↑ Higher is better

A completed encounter is a patient visit that has a signed clinical note in the EHR. Encounters are counted per provider per clinical week and normalized to each provider's scheduled clinical hours to account for part-time work. The metric captures whether providers can see more patients as documentation burden decreases after Suki adoption.

Gold Standard
Completed, signed encounters from the EHR, normalized to scheduled clinical hours per week. Preferred — captures realized patient throughput and controls for part-time schedules.
Default / Practical
Scheduled encounters per week (from the EHR schedule), when completion and signing status are unavailable. Use with caution — scheduled volume does not equal completed volume and may not reflect the documentation-driven capacity effect.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Normalize for scheduled clinical hours. A provider who reduces from 5 to 4 clinical days will show a raw encounter drop unrelated to Suki. Divide by scheduled hours or clinical days to compute a rate; compare rates, not raw counts.
  • Exclude providers who changed practice context. Providers who changed specialty, site, or panel size during the analysis window introduce confounders that are unrelated to Suki adoption. Exclude these providers or analyze them separately.
  • Only include completed, signed encounters. Scheduled-but-not-completed visits (no-shows, cancellations) and encounters without a signed note should be excluded. Mixing completion statuses inflates raw counts and obscures the capacity signal.
Interpretation caution: Increased encounter volume is not universally desirable. Higher throughput achieved through documentation efficiency gains is a positive signal, but it should be interpreted alongside workload indicators and care quality measures. A provider seeing more patients without a corresponding reduction in documentation time (CM-04) warrants investigation.

Algorithm

1 Dashboard Metric — Cohort Average For the Suki admin console

Produces the average completed encounters per provider per week in the cohort for a given month. This number is expected to increase after Suki adoption as time freed from documentation creates capacity for additional patient visits. Holmgren 2026 found +0.80 completed encounters/week in ambulatory settings.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
Completed encounters per provider per week
The canonical unit for this measure — defined in the Unit card above. Computed per provider per calendar month, then averaged across the cohort.
Secondary
Active providers in the cohort
The denominator. An active provider is one who has completed at least one Suki session in the reporting month and has passed their onboarding ramp period (default: 30 days post-adoption).
⚠ During the onboarding ramp, some providers may temporarily see fewer patients while adapting to Suki. Exclude the ramp period to avoid a false-negative signal in the early post-adoption months.
Cohort Average=∑ (avg completed encounters / clinical week per provider, in the month)Number of active providers in the cohort
Per-provider weekly rate = total completed encounters in month M / clinical weeks in month M Clinical weeks should be normalized to scheduled clinical hours where available Reporting grain: one value per cohort per calendar month Pre / post boundary: each provider's Suki adoption date (first completed session) Exclude: providers still within their onboarding ramp period (default 30 days)
Pseudocode
For each calendar month M: active_providers = providers with ≥1 completed Suki session AND past ramp period For each provider p in active_providers: completed_enc[p] = completed, signed encounters for provider p in month M clinical_weeks[p] = scheduled clinical weeks (or days/5) for provider p in month M enc_per_week[p] = completed_enc[p] / clinical_weeks[p] dashboard_value[M] = MEAN( enc_per_week[p] for p in active_providers )
Expected Trend — Completed Encounters / Provider / Week / Month

Cohort average should increase after Suki adoption as documentation burden decreases and capacity is released. Holmgren 2026: +0.80 encounters/week (ambulatory, statistically significant). Shuaib 2021 reports +39% patients/hour with human scribes (ED) — an upper-bound comparator from an adjacent intervention type.

Data Requirements

Data Element Description Source Notes
Total completed encounters per provider/month Count of signed, completed encounters per provider per calendar month. The primary numerator for this measure. EHR Epic: total_encounters  |  Cerner: total_encounters. Include only encounters with a signed note. Exclude no-shows and cancellations.
Avg completed encounters per working day An alternative denominator-normalized form. Use when scheduled hours data are unavailable. EHR Epic: encounters_per_day  |  Cerner: encounters_per_day. Consistent with per-week normalization when multiplied by 5.
Scheduled clinical hours Each provider's scheduled clinical time per week. Used to normalize encounter counts for part-time providers. EHR / HR Available from EHR scheduling module or HR system. Critical for fair comparison across full-time and part-time providers.
Provider specialty Provider specialty code. Used to stratify results and exclude providers who changed specialty during the analysis window. EHR Encounter volume norms vary substantially by specialty. Report results by specialty when the cohort is mixed.
Provider identifiers Consistent provider IDs linking EHR encounter records to Suki session records. EHR Suki Critical join key. Must be reconciled between systems before analysis.
Suki adoption date Date of each provider's first completed Suki session. Defines the pre/post boundary. Suki Without this, there is no pre/post split.
Active usage flag (monthly) Confirmation that the provider had at least one completed Suki session in a given month. Defines the active-provider denominator. Suki Session count per week can also serve to flag whether an individual week qualifies as an “active Suki use” week, enabling week-level rather than month-level pre/post splits.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki identifies which weeks are active-use weeks, but encounter counts come entirely from the EHR. The Suki session count per week provides a finer-grained signal than the adoption date alone: a provider who used Suki in 3 out of 4 weeks in a month can be analyzed at the week level, with the 3 Suki weeks as “post-adoption” and the 1 non-Suki week as a within-provider control.

This week-level design is stronger than a simple pre/post month comparison for short analysis windows. It requires Suki session data at the weekly grain and matched encounter data from the EHR at the same grain.
