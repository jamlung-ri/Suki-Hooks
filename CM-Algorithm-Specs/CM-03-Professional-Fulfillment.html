Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-03 — Professional Fulfillment and Meaningfulness

NB — Net Benefits Provider Wellbeing Secondary / Exploratory Also known as: Meaning in Work  ·  PFI Professional Fulfillment Subscale

Unit

Unit
PFI Professional Fulfillment subscale score (1–5)
↑ Higher is better

Provider-reported positive engagement with clinical work — the presence of meaning, reward, and satisfaction in the work itself. This is the positive pole of the wellbeing spectrum, dissociable from CM-01 (absence of exhaustion). Removing a negative (burnout) does not imply adding a positive (fulfillment), and the strongest available evidence shows exactly this dissociation.

Gold Standard
Stanford Professional Fulfillment Index (PFI), Professional Fulfillment subscale — 6-item, 1–5 scale (higher = better). The instrument used in Afshar 2025b. If a full PFI is administered for CM-01, this subscale is collected "for free" in the same instrument.
Default / Practical
Custom meaningfulness-of-work and work-life balance Likert items (Afshar 2025b, Boyter 2025, Duggan 2025) — conceptually adjacent supplements with weaker instrument validation; not a substitute for the PFI subscale.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Expected null result. Afshar 2025b (RCT, n=66) found professional fulfillment increased at p=0.04, which missed the pre-specified alpha of 0.025, while burnout (CM-01) decreased significantly (−0.44). The expected outcome for CM-03 is a null result — this should be stated up front in any report, not discovered after the fact.
  • Low actionability. The H1 deep-dive scores CM-03 as Tier 3 (7/12), driven by Low Actionability — no current Suki hook is designed to act on meaning, reward, or engagement (unlike CM-01/CM-02, where H1/H8/H10/H11 plausibly act on burden or cognitive-load mechanisms). Even a clean measurement gives no lever to pull.
  • Do not separately scope. If a full Stanford PFI is administered for CM-01, the Professional Fulfillment subscale is collected in the same instrument at no additional respondent burden. Standing up a separate CM-03 data-collection effort is not recommended.
  • Framing risk. Reporting CM-03 as a standalone, primary value-proposition metric risks being read as "Suki doesn't improve wellbeing" when the more accurate reading is "Suki improves one wellbeing construct (burnout) and not a different, dissociable one (fulfillment)."

Algorithm

1 Survey Instrument Scoring — Bundled Dissociation Check Same administration as CM-01 PFI

Produces a cohort-level professional-fulfillment score from the same Phase 0 / Phase 3 PFI administration used for CM-01, reported alongside the CM-01 burnout delta as a dissociation check, not a standalone outcome. The expected result is a flat or non-significant change in this score even where CM-01's burnout score decreases.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
PFI Professional Fulfillment subscale score (1–5)
The canonical unit for this measure — defined in the Unit card above. Computed as the mean of the 6 subscale items per respondent per administration wave, then averaged within each utilization-tier cohort. Collected from the same survey instance as CM-01's Work Exhaustion subscale.
Secondary
Suki utilization tier (heavy / moderate / light)
Identical cohort definition to CM-01 — heavy (≥70% of eligible encounters with a completed Suki session), moderate (30–69%), light (<30%), assigned per provider by week 4 of adoption. Reuse the CM-01 tier assignment; do not recompute separately.
Secondary
CM-01 Work Exhaustion delta (for dissociation comparison)
The same respondents' CM-01 burnout-score change, reported side-by-side with the CM-03 fulfillment-score change. The comparison itself — not either score alone — is the CM-03 finding: did burnout improve while fulfillment did not (replicating Afshar 2025b), or did both move together?
⚠ A significant CM-03 increase would be a notable departure from the strongest prior RCT and should be reported carefully — not assumed to be a measurement artifact, but also not over-claimed from a single deployment.

Pseudocode
Phase 0 (T0, pre-deployment, same respondents as CM-01): fulfillment[p, T0] = MEAN(PFI Professional Fulfillment items 1..6) for provider p Phase 3 (T3, months 4-9, same respondents where possible): fulfillment[p, T3] = MEAN(PFI Professional Fulfillment items 1..6) for provider p tier[p] = HEAVY / MODERATE / LIGHT (reuse CM-01 tier assignment, week 4) For each tier T in {HEAVY, MODERATE, LIGHT}: cohort_fulfillment[T, T0] = MEAN(fulfillment[p, T0] for p in tier T) cohort_fulfillment[T, T3] = MEAN(fulfillment[p, T3] for p in tier T) fulfillment_delta[T] = cohort_fulfillment[T, T3] - cohort_fulfillment[T, T0] # Dissociation check: compare against CM-01's exhaustion_delta[T] for the same tier T dashboard_value = (fulfillment_delta[T], exhaustion_delta[T]) for T = HEAVY Expected: exhaustion_delta < 0 (improvement) AND fulfillment_delta ~ 0 (null)
Expected Trend — Dissociation: CM-01 Burnout vs. CM-03 Fulfillment

The expected pattern is dissociation: the CM-01 Work Exhaustion score (left) declines for the heavy-utilization cohort, while the CM-03 Professional Fulfillment score (right) stays flat. A flat right panel is the expected, reportable result — not a failed measurement.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's role for CM-03 is identical to CM-01 — adoption-boundary and utilization-cohort scaffolding only. CM-03 has no distinct Suki-native data requirement and no product mechanism currently targets this construct. Suki cannot observe "meaning" or "reward" directly, and there is no in-app survey-distribution capability for either measure.

CM-03 should be measured only as a secondary/exploratory outcome bundled with CM-01's PFI administration, framed explicitly as a test of the burnout/fulfillment dissociation finding — not as a standalone value-proposition metric, and not as the basis for sample-size or power calculations.
