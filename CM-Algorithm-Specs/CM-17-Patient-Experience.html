Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-17 — Patient Experience and Relationship Quality

OI — Organizational Impact Tier 3 — 5/12 Also known as: Patient-Doctor Relationship Quality (PDRQ-9)  ·  Patient Satisfaction / Press Ganey  ·  Likelihood to Recommend (LTR)

Unit

Unit
PDRQ-9 score (9-item, 1–5)
↑ Higher is better

The patient's own report of the quality of the patient-physician interaction — relationship quality, satisfaction with the visit, and the experience of being heard. This is the most underdeveloped outcome domain in the entire canonical measures corpus: only Owens 2024 has used a validated patient-reported instrument, and it found a null result in the masked phase (p=.31) — the open-label phase was positive, more consistent with a patient-expectation effect than a true relationship-quality effect.

Gold Standard
PDRQ-9 (Patient-Doctor Relationship Questionnaire) — 9-item validated patient-reported scale, 1–5 per item, higher = better. The only validated instrument used for CM-17 in the corpus (Owens 2024).
Default / Practical
Site-administered patient satisfaction survey (Press Ganey or equivalent) or a single Likelihood-to-Recommend (LTR, NPS-style) item — lower marginal cost where a site already runs one, but generally not validated against the specific "AI scribe present" condition.
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • Weak prior evidence. A single validated-instrument study (Owens 2024) found a null result in the masked phase. CM-17 should not be a standalone primary outcome — a general-population study risks merely replicating this null, not adding new information.
  • Patient-survey-distribution gap. Unlike CM-01/02/03's clinician-survey gap, CM-17 requires reaching patients — a different population with different consent and contact requirements. Suki has no mechanism to reach patients at all today.
  • The Multilingual Opportunity reframes the priority. Rather than a low-powered general-population study, the canonical measures corpus's hook-matrix Sprint 2B pairs H4 (multilingual) + CM-17 via PDRQ-9 in a multilingual patient cohort — an unstudied mechanism with zero published evidence and an existing Suki-native cohort-identification signal (see Algorithm below).
  • Session-level language summary missing. lang_id exists per transcript segment, but there is no session-level "primary patient language ≠ English" summary field — the multilingual cohort currently requires post-processing every transcript.
  • Patient consent/opt-out capture missing. One of the five defined CM-17 methods ("Patient Opt-Out Rate," "Patient consent compliance") is purely operational, but no consent-capture field exists in the current Suki API surface.

Algorithm

1 Survey Instrument Scoring — Targeted PDRQ-9 in Multilingual Cohort (H4 Pathway) Sprint 2B framing — H4 + CM-17

Rather than a broad, low-powered general-population CM-17 study, concentrates survey effort on the multilingual-encounter cohort — identifiable today from transcript lang_id with no new instrumentation — and compares PDRQ-9 distributions between encounters where the patient spoke a language other than English (a plausible, previously untested relationship-quality mechanism: the physician listens in the patient's own language rather than typing/translating) and English-only encounters.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
PDRQ-9 score (9-item, 1–5, higher = better)
Distributed post-visit to patients in the identified cohort. Computed as the mean of the 9 items per respondent, then averaged within each cohort (multilingual vs. English-only).
Secondary
Multilingual-encounter cohort flag (lang_id ≠ "en")
Derived from transcript segment language identification — flags encounters where any meaningful portion of the conversation was in a language other than English. This is the cohort-defining variable for the comparison.
⚠ Currently requires per-segment post-processing of every transcript; a session-level summary field ("primary patient language") would make this cohort trivial to construct at scale.
Secondary
Suki utilization tier (heavy / moderate / light)
Standard cohort definition (as CM-01/02/03/06) — included as a covariate so any PDRQ-9 difference between language cohorts isn't confounded with overall Suki exposure.
PDRQ-9 Score=∑ (item response, 1–5) across 9 items9
Cohort definition: multilingual (lang_id != "en" on >= 1 transcript segment) vs. English-only encounters Administration: PDRQ-9 distributed post-visit to patients in both cohorts (external mechanism, see Scope below) Reporting: cohort-mean PDRQ-9 score, multilingual vs. English-only, with Suki utilization tier as covariate Fallback path: if PDRQ-9 distribution is infeasible, request a pre/post or AI-exposed/not-exposed breakout from the site's existing Press Ganey (or equivalent) data, segmented by the same multilingual-cohort flag where the site's survey data includes a language field
Pseudocode
For each session s: langs[s] = SET(segment.lang_id for segment in s.transcript) cohort[s] = "multilingual" if (langs[s] - {"en"}) is non-empty else "english_only" tier[s] = utilization_tier(s.provider) # heavy / moderate / light, as in CM-01/02/03 # External step: distribute PDRQ-9 post-visit to patients in sessions s # (requires patient contact info + consent — see Data Requirements) for response in pdrq9_responses: score[response] = MEAN(item_1..item_9) for cohort_name in {"multilingual", "english_only"}: cohort_scores = [score[r] for r in pdrq9_responses if cohort[r.session] == cohort_name] dashboard_value[cohort_name] = MEAN(cohort_scores) # Primary comparison: dashboard_value["multilingual"] vs dashboard_value["english_only"] # Expected: multilingual cohort shows a larger positive effect (novel mechanism, no prior null to replicate)
Expected Trend — PDRQ-9 by Language Cohort

Because the general-population CM-17 effect already has a published null (Owens 2024, masked phase, p=.31), the expected pattern is not a large effect across the whole population. The multilingual cohort is where a meaningful, previously untested effect is plausible — the comparison itself, not either bar alone, is the finding.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's role for CM-17 is limited to cohort-identification scaffolding — specifically, the multilingual-encounter cohort derivable today from transcript lang_id, plus the standard utilization-tier cohort used across CM-01/02/03/06. Suki has no native patient-survey-distribution mechanism and no patient consent/opt-out capture — both are fundamentally different gaps from the clinician-survey gaps in CM-01/02/03, since they involve reaching and consenting a different population (patients).

Given the single published null result (Owens 2024), CM-17 should not be powered as a standalone general-population outcome. The most actionable path is the targeted PDRQ-9 multilingual-cohort design above (Sprint 2B); the fallback is piggybacking on a site's existing Press Ganey/patient-satisfaction infrastructure for a pre/post breakout with no new instrumentation.
