Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-02 — Cognitive and Task Load

NB — Net Benefits Provider Wellbeing Also known as: Mental Workload  ·  NASA-TLX  ·  Physician Task Load

Unit

Unit
NASA-TLX / PTL score (0–100)
↓ Lower is better

The mental effort, time pressure, and frustration a clinician experiences during or immediately after a clinical encounter — the in-the-moment cognitive demand of documentation, measured via the NASA Task Load Index or a brief physician-specific adaptation. Distinct from CM-01 (sustained emotional exhaustion over weeks-to-months) and CM-04 (objective documentation duration).

Gold Standard
NASA-TLX (full) — 6-domain instrument (Mental Demand, Physical Demand, Temporal Demand, Performance, Effort, Frustration), 0–100 per subscale. Used in Bracken 2025, PHTI 2025, Omon 2025, Stults 2025. Frustration and Temporal Demand subscales are most relevant to documentation-specific burden.
Default / Practical
Physician Task Load Index (PTL) — 4-item NASA-TLX adaptation, 0–100 scale (lower = better), used in Shah 2024. Lower respondent burden, suitable for repeated or frequent administration (e.g., end-of-shift).
⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • The timing problem. NASA-TLX validity depends on proximity to the encounter — ideally administered immediately post-encounter, not weeks later. Suki has no mechanism to trigger a micro-survey on session completion, so administration falls back to end-of-shift or periodic, which introduces recall aggregation across multiple encounters.
  • Composite vs. subscale. Full NASA-TLX (6 domains) vs. Frustration/Temporal Demand subscales only vs. PTL (4 items) are not interchangeable scores. Pick one operationalization and apply it consistently across the comparison window.
  • Pathway attribution. Four distinct hooks (H1 ambient documentation, H8 orders staging, H10 pre-visit summaries, H11 chart Q&A) plausibly reduce cognitive load through different mechanisms. A NASA-TLX change cannot, by itself, identify which pathway drove it — pathway isolation requires session-level exposure flags not yet available.

Algorithm

1 Survey Instrument Scoring — End-of-Shift Dose-Response Comparison Periodic administration

Produces a cohort-level workload score, compared between heavy- and light-utilization Suki users at the same point in time (dose-response), since a true pre/post comparison requires the same pre-deployment baseline timing discipline as CM-01. The score is expected to be lower (PTL/NASA-TLX scale) in heavier-utilization cohorts.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
NASA-TLX / PTL score (0–100)
The canonical unit for this measure — defined in the Unit card above. Computed per respondent per administration (end-of-shift or per-encounter where feasible), then averaged within each utilization-tier cohort for the reporting period.
⚠ Trigger mechanism not available: session completion (status = completed) is, in principle, exactly the timestamped event a per-encounter micro-survey would need, but no in-app or webhook-based trigger exists today. Per-encounter administration is aspirational; end-of-shift is the practical default.
Secondary
Suki utilization tier (heavy / moderate / light)
Same PHTI 2025 tiering used in CM-01: heavy (≥70% of eligible encounters with a completed Suki session), moderate (30–69%), light (<30%), assigned per provider by week 4 of adoption.
Secondary
Cognitive-offload proxy (note content volume + structured codes)
Number and length of Suki-generated note sections, and count of auto-generated ICD-10/IMO codes per encounter — a rough proxy for documentation work removed from the clinician. Reported as a secondary, corroborating signal alongside the survey score, not as a substitute.
⚠ Does not measure perceived effort directly. An elevated skipped/failed session rate for a provider may also flag sessions where ambient capture itself added friction (a frustration signal) rather than reducing it.
Cohort Score=∑ (PTL or NASA-TLX score per respondent, in the wave)Number of respondents in the cohort
PTL score = MEAN(4 items) × 25 (0-100 scale, lower = better) NASA-TLX composite = MEAN(6 subscales, each 0-100); or report Frustration / Temporal Demand subscales individually Administration: end-of-shift or periodic (per-encounter aspirational, pending trigger mechanism) Cohort: heavy / moderate / light Suki utilization tier (PHTI 2025), assigned at week 4 Corroboration: report alongside cognitive-offload proxy (note content volume, structured codes) where available
Pseudocode
For each reporting wave W (end-of-shift or periodic): For each respondent p: if PTL administered: score[p, W] = MEAN(PTL items 1..4) * 25 if NASA-TLX administered: score[p, W] = MEAN(subscale_1..6) # or report Frustration/Temporal Demand alone tier[p] = HEAVY / MODERATE / LIGHT (per CM-01 utilization tiering, week-4 snapshot) For each tier T in {HEAVY, MODERATE, LIGHT}: cohort_score[T, W] = MEAN(score[p, W] for p in tier T) dashboard_value[W] = cohort_score[HEAVY, W] vs cohort_score[LIGHT, W] (expect HEAVY cohort score lower, i.e. less workload, than LIGHT cohort) Secondary (corroboration, per encounter): offload_proxy = note_section_count + structured_code_count (from Suki content/structured-data)
Expected Trend — NASA-TLX / PTL Score / Cohort / Wave

Cohort-mean workload score should be lower in the heavy-utilization cohort than the light-utilization cohort at the same point in time, and should decline for the heavy cohort across waves as familiarity with ambient capture increases. Without a pre-deployment baseline, the comparison is heavy-vs-light at a given wave, not a within-provider pre/post change.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki's role for CM-02 is limited to utilization-cohort scaffolding and cognitive-offload proxies. Session counts define the dose-response cohorts, and note content volume / structured-code counts provide a rough, secondary signal for documentation work removed from the clinician. Suki has no in-session or immediate post-session micro-survey mechanism, so it cannot natively trigger the NASA-TLX/PTL administration that the instrument's validity depends on.

NASA-TLX or PTL responses must be collected externally (end-of-shift or periodic), on a heavy-vs-light cohort comparison basis. Report the offload proxy alongside the survey score as corroborating evidence, not as a substitute for it.
