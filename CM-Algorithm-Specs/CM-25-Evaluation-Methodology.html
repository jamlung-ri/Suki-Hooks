Canonical Measure Algorithm Card

CM-25 — Evaluation Methodology Quality

Meta-Measure — Not a D&M Outcome Literature-Corpus Quality Control ⚠ Candidate for Removal — Team Decision Pending Also known as: Inter-Rater Reliability (ICC)  ·  Evidence-Design Tier  ·  Study Rigor Score  ·  Methodological Quality

Unit

Unit
Study-rigor profile — Inter-rater reliability (ICC) + Evidence-design tier
↑ Higher reliability / rigor is better

A composite indicator of how trustworthy a given published study’s measurement approach is — combining (a) inter-rater reliability, typically the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) or RWG agreement statistic among independent human/LLM raters, and (b) an evidence-design tier ranking the study’s methodological rigor (e.g., RCT > propensity-matched observational > pre-post QI study with no control).

This is not a property of Suki’s product or of any clinical outcome — it is a property of how a given source paper in the literature review was conducted. It scores the evidence, not Suki.

⚠ Ambiguities to Resolve
  • AI-derived categorization, human judgment required. The corpus’s own Key Note is explicit that methodology-quality assessment should be conducted by the human research team, not generated by AI. The content on this card documents the mechanics used elsewhere in the review (ICC, evidence tiering) — the team should own all final quality judgments, not treat this card’s formula as a substitute for expert review.
  • No single accepted composite formula. ICC and evidence-design tier are measured on different scales (a continuous 0–1 statistic vs. an ordinal category) and the corpus does not specify a validated way to combine them into one score. Treat “study-rigor profile” as a reporting convention — report both components side by side — not a validated single number.
  • Applies to source papers, not to Suki. Nothing on this page describes Suki’s product or clinical quality — it describes how rigorously a cited study measured something else (documentation time, note accuracy, burnout, etc.). Confirm this is understood before this card circulates outside the review team.
⚠ Removal-Candidate Flag

This measure was flagged as a candidate for removal from the canonical set — it evaluates study rigor, not a Suki product/clinical outcome, and does not fit the D&M framework as an AI-derived category. Retained here as a literature-corpus quality-control mechanism pending a team decision on whether to keep it as CM-25 or fold its mechanics directly into each paper’s record in the review database instead.

Algorithm

1 Study Rigor Profile — ICC + Evidence-Design Tier Applied to literature-review source papers, not to Suki sessions

Scores a source paper’s methodological trustworthiness on two independent axes: statistical agreement among the paper’s own raters (ICC), and a design-tier ranking of the study’s overall rigor. Used internally to weight how much confidence to place in a paper’s reported effect sizes when synthesizing across the literature-review corpus — it is not computed from any Suki product or clinical data.

Units in this Algorithm
Primary
Inter-rater reliability (ICC)
Continuous 0–1 statistic, computed per source paper across its own reported independent raters (human and/or LLM-as-judge). Not reported by every paper — only those that used multi-rater scoring.
Secondary
Evidence-design tier
Ordinal ranking of the study’s design (RCT > propensity-matched observational > pre-post QI, no control > case report / simulation-only), assigned during literature-review coding.
⚠ Tier assignment is a human-review judgment call for borderline designs (e.g., a QI study with partial matching) — not a mechanical function of reported keywords.
Secondary
Cross-replicate consistency
Where reported (e.g., Kernberg 2023: 53%), the stability of a paper’s measures across repeated model runs on the same input — an additional rigor signal specific to papers evaluating LLM-generated content.
ICC (two-way random)=Between-Rater VarianceBetween-Rater Variance + Within-Rater (Residual) Variance
Standard two-way random-effects ICC (Shrout & Fleiss 1979 / McGraw & Wong 1996 form) Computed per source paper, across that paper's own reported raters Reporting grain: one ICC value (where available) + one evidence-tier label, per source paper
Pseudocode — Rigor Profile Assembly (mechanics only, not a combined score)
for paper P in corpus: # Component 1: inter-rater reliability (only if P reports independent raters) if P.reports_multi_rater_scores: icc[P] = ICC_TWO_WAY_RANDOM(P.rater_scores) # or RWG, if P used a within-group agreement design else: icc[P] = NOT_REPORTED # Component 2: evidence-design tier (human-assigned during literature-review coding) tier[P] = CASE WHEN P.design == "RCT" (incl. stepped-wedge) THEN 1 # highest rigor WHEN P.design == "propensity-matched observational" THEN 2 WHEN P.design == "pre-post QI, no control" THEN 3 WHEN P.design IN ("case report", "simulation-only") THEN 4 # lowest rigor ELSE tier[P] = REQUIRES_HUMAN_REVIEW # ambiguous design # Component 3 (where reported): cross-replicate consistency consistency[P] = P.cross_replicate_consistency_pct OR NOT_REPORTED # This algorithm deliberately does NOT auto-combine these into one number. # Per the Key Note above, the human research team reviews the profile as a whole. rigor_profile[P] = { icc: icc[P], design_tier: tier[P], cross_replicate: consistency[P] }
Expected Trend — Not Applicable

There is no pre/post Suki-adoption axis for this measure. CM-25 scores a source paper’s study rigor at the time it was published — it does not track anything that changes as a Suki customer adopts or matures on the product. No trend chart applies.

Data Requirements

Data Element Description Source Notes
Study design classification The source paper's reported design (RCT, propensity-matched observational, pre-post QI, case report, simulation-only). — Read from the paper’s Methods section and captured during literature-review coding — not from Suki or EHR data.
Rater scores (2+ independent raters, human and/or LLM-as-judge) Per-item or per-note scores from independent raters, where the paper reports them. Basis for the ICC calculation. Human Rater Only available for papers that report multi-rater scoring (e.g., Kernberg 2023, Foo 2025); most corpus papers do not report inter-rater statistics at all.
Sample size / control-group description Number of encounters/notes evaluated and whether a comparison or control group was used. — Literature-review coding field; supports the evidence-tier assignment.
Benchmark dataset availability flag Whether the paper's evaluation used a publicly available benchmark dataset. — Per Gebauer 2025's scoping review of benchmarking datasets in this domain.
Cross-replicate consistency (where reported) % of data elements consistent across repeated model runs on the same input. — Reported only by Kernberg 2023 (53%) in the current corpus.

Suki Data — Scoped Role

Suki data has no role in this measure. CM-25 evaluates the methodological rigor of a cited source paper’s own study design — it is entirely internal to the literature-review process and does not draw on any Suki API, EHR, or product telemetry.

Suki’s product and clinical data are the subject being evaluated by the other canonical measures (CM-01 through CM-24); CM-25 evaluates the trustworthiness of the published evidence about those subjects, independent of Suki. If this measure is retained, it should stay scoped to the literature-review team’s own workflow and should not appear in any Suki-facing dashboard or customer-reporting context.
